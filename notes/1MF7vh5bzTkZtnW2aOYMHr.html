The world

Manchester, January 1980: Factory FACT 14, The Return of the Durutti Column, Martin Hannett producing, in a sandpaper sleeve glued together by members of Joy Division — and inside it a guitar played through delay and reverb until it stopped being riffs and became weather. This list is that sound and its family tree: the two ancestors who taught a guitar to sustain (Robert Fripp's tape loops with Eno on Evening Star, 1975; Bill Frisell's ECM debut In Line, recorded 1982, released 1983), the 1984–89 Britain that grew up around Vini — Maurice Deebank's classical-guitar Felt on Cherry Red, Johnny Marr's one Smiths instrumental, New Order's "Elegia" for Ian Curtis, Dif Juz on 4AD under Robin Guthrie's production, the Blue Nile's rain-lit Glasgow, and Morrissey hiring Vini himself to play on Viva Hate — and the descendants: Slowdive's Creation-era delay, Mick Turner's guitar in the Dirty Three, Neil Young improvising Dead Man on Old Black while the film ran, Loren Connors' cassette-recorded Airs, Mogwai, and the pedal-steel-as-weather line of Daniel Lanois and Chuck Johnson, with Marc Ribot's solo silent-film pieces between them. It ends where Andrew has been going since summer: Renascent (London Records / Fact 2.51, 31 July 2026), the first new Durutti Column album in sixteen years.

Connective tissue & sequencing

It opens on the door — "Sketch for Summer", the one he ♥'d in real time — then steps back to the two ancestors, Fripp's tape delay (1975) and Frisell's ECM reverb (1983): the two ways a guitar learned to hold a note. From there the British 1984–89 block runs Manchester-outward: Felt → the Smiths → New Order → Dif Juz → the Blue Nile → Morrissey with Vini on guitar → "People's Pleasure Park" (1989), so Vini's own decade is bookended by his debut's descendants and his own return. Slowdive carries the delay into the nineties, and "Sketch for Winter" is the hinge — summer opens the list, winter turns it — into the cold, abstract run where the guitar is barely a song at all: Dirty Three, Neil Young's "Guitar Solo, No. 1", Loren Connors' two-minute "Airs 4". Mogwai's murmured vocal lets a voice back in, and the last stretch hands the sustain to pedal steel (Lanois, Chuck Johnson) with Ribot's "Delancey Waltz" between them. It closes on "Time Present and Time Past" (2026) — the record he has been reaching for since July, and a title that is the shape of the whole list. About 99 minutes.
