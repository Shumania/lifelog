The world

Nine seeds, but really one lineage: three generations of smart, talk-sung guitar music passed from papa to kid. The 90s wing is wry and slack — Pavement's shrugged brilliance, Cake's deadpan trumpet-funk, Elliott Smith whispering in the corner. The 2000s wing is communal and kinetic — Toronto's Arts & Crafts collective (Broken Social Scene and its orbit) and DFA-era New York, where LCD Soundsystem turned record-collector anxiety into dance music. The current wing is the slacker revival that inherited all of it: Courtney Barnett and Julia Jacklin's plainspoken Australian songwriting, Geese's unhinged NYC art-rock. Different decades, same DNA: dry wit, loose guitars, singers who talk more than they sing until the feelings suddenly arrive.

Selection method

19 seed tracks / 13 adjacents (59/41). Seeds got two apiece (Elliott Smith three — he carries the quiet act of the sequence). Adjacents were chosen by verifiable real-world connections, not similar-artist vibes: Feist and Metric (Emily Haines) are literal Broken Social Scene members/collaborators from the You Forgot It in People era; Stephen Malkmus is Pavement's own frontman solo; Kurt Vile duets with Barnett on Lotta Sea Lice ("Over Everything" is the bridge itself); Talking Heads are LCD Soundsystem's acknowledged ancestor; Parquet Courts sit exactly between Pavement's talk-sing and LCD's NYC dance-punk; Spoon shares Cake's wry minimalism and Ben Kweller's Austin; Snail Mail and Big Thief are Jacklin's confessional-indie peers; Yo La Tengo's "Autumn Sweater" is the hinge into Elliott Smith's hush; The Breeders, Modest Mouse and Wilco anchor the 90s–00s alt canon between Pavement and the moderns. All 32 tracks resolved via Spotify search with exact artist+title verification; anchors here are the seed-artist warhorses ("The Distance", "Cut Your Hair", "All My Friends", "Between the Bars") with deeper cuts around them.

Connective tissue & sequencing

Opens punchy and funny — "Short Skirt/Long Jacket" into Spoon's "The Way We Get By" into "Wasted & Ready" — then a 90s slacker run ("Cut Your Hair", "Cannonball", "Pedestrian at Best"). Geese hand off to Parquet Courts and the NYC stretch peaks with "Daft Punk Is Playing at My House" and its ancestor "This Must Be the Place". A singalong heart ("Float On", "The Distance", "Gold Soundz") melts into the slacker-drawl valley — Malkmus solo, the Barnett/Vile duet, "Avant Gardener", Wilco, Kweller — before one last rowdy kick ("3D Country", "Combat Baby") lifts into the Toronto uplift of "7/4 (Shoreline)" and "Mushaboom". From there it's the long soft landing: Big Thief, Snail Mail, both Jacklin songs, "Autumn Sweater", and the Elliott Smith trio wrapped around "Anthems for a Seventeen Year-Old Girl" — which is, after all, a song for a girl to grow into. It closes with "All My Friends": the papa song, the one about time passing with the people you love, built to be played loud in a backyard in August.
