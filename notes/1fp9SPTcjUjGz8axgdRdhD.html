The world

The American underground folk circle of roughly 2003–2007 — the press called it freak folk or New Weird America — drawn the way its own members drew it: Banhart's Golden Apples of the Sun comp (2004) and Gnomonsong, the label he founded with Vetiver's Andy Cabic in 2005. The hubs are labels and living rooms: Michael Gira's Young God (Banhart's first three records, Akron/Family), Drag City (Six Organs, Newsom, Jansch's Black Swan, P.G. Six), Locust in Philadelphia (Espers, Josephine Foster), K in Olympia (Little Wings), Gnomonsong (Jana Hunter's debut was release #1; Hurley's Ancestral Swamp came two years later). Geography runs San Francisco's Mission (Banhart and Cabic, 2002–05) → Nevada City (Newsom, Alela Diane) → Philadelphia (Espers) → Houston (Jana Hunter) → Brooklyn (Akron/Family, CocoRosie, Antony and Joan). The scene's defining habit was sitting at its elders' feet — getting Vashti Bunyan, Linda Perhacs, Michael Hurley and Bert Jansch back into studios and, often, onto the same tape.

Seven tracks are by Golden Apples artists; the cuts are the comp's own where Spotify has them (Vetiver, Little Wings, Josephine Foster) and the artist's strongest album cut where it doesn't (Six Organs' "Hazy SF" and Espers' "Byss & Abyss" are the comp picks; "Home" and "Flowery Noontide" stand in). Joanna Newsom (comp #2, "Bridges and Balloons") isn't on Spotify at all — her entire catalogue — so she's present only as the harp on Vashti's Lookaftering and on Vetiver's Amerilie. Karen Dalton's 2006 Light in the Attic reissue (Banhart wrote the notes) was considered and held: Gate of Horn already earmarked her for a soul night, and "Katie Cruel" here is Jansch's reading of her song.

Connective tissue & sequencing

  • Opens with the seed album's own first track — the Cap Hill sitting started here on Monday.
  • The Bay and the comp (2–6): Vetiver's "Angels' Share" is Golden Apples track 1 (Cabic co-founded Gnomonsong with Banhart; Hope Sandoval of Mazzy Star sings the harmony) → Little Wings (comp #16; Kyle Field has played in Banhart's band, and Feist later named a film after the song) → Iron & Wine (comp #11 is "Fever Dream"; the anchor "Naked as We Came" is the same 2004 record) → Alela Diane, recorded by her father in Nevada City in the summer of 2004 and reissued by Holocene → Six Organs (Ben Chasny, comp #3; "Home" is School of the Flower, Drag City 2005).
  • The elders (7–11), as the scene recorded them: the hinge is the duet — "Rejoicing in the Hands" is Banhart literally handing the song to Vashti Bunyan (Young God, 2004; also comp #7). She answers with her own "Here Before" from Lookaftering (FatCat, 2005): Max Richter producing, Banhart on guitar, Newsom on harp. Linda Perhacs' "Chimacum Rain" (1970) is a Washington song — Chimacum sits on the Olympic Peninsula — reissued by The Wild Places in 2003 and then she sang on Banhart's "Freely" in 2007. Michael Hurley's "Knockando" is from The Ancestral Swamp, the record Gnomonsong put out with him in 2007; he'd long since settled in Oregon, near Astoria. Bert Jansch's "Katie Cruel" (The Black Swan, Drag City 2006) has Beth Orton and Banhart on vocals and Vetiver/Espers drummer Otto Hauser behind them — the Soho elder of Witchseason meeting the kids.
  • East and south (12–18): Espers out of Philadelphia (Greg Weeks, Meg Baird; comp #6) → Josephine Foster (comp #5, the same "Little Life") → Jana Hunter from Houston (Gnomonsong #1, 2005; "Farm, Ca.") → Akron/Family (Young God, 2005, produced by Gira) → Antony's "Hope There's Someone" (Banhart sings on the same album's "Spiralling"; Joan Wasser on viola) → Joan and Antony trading verses on "I Defy" (Real Life, 2006–07) → CocoRosie, the Casady sisters' Paris-bathroom record (Touch and Go, 2004); Antony sang on their next one.
  • Where the hush went (19–22): Brightblack Morning Light (Matador, 2006 — Shineywater's Quiet Color festival hosted Newsom and Banhart) → the two Vashon anchors, P.G. Six (Pat Gubler, ex-Tower Recordings with the comp's Matt Valentine; Drag City 2011) and Cass McCombs (Domino 2011, a Bay Area contemporary of Banhart's from the A years) → closes on Jessica Pratt's "Night Faces" (San Francisco, 2012): a record that could have been comp track 21 eight years earlier — the scene's sound outliving the scene.
