The world

This isn't one scene; it's a map of where the curiosity went over four months, re-read at low volume. The through-line is stillness with a pulse: a Dowland pavan for viols (1604, recorded 1993) sits beside a 1959 Bill Evans ballad and John Lewis's chamber-jazz reading of Ornette; Brahem's oud-clarinet-bass trio and Mulatu's tezeta (the Amharic blues mode of longing) hand off to Nusrat's qawwali as Bristol slowed it down in 1990. From there it's American soul in its quietest register — Withers' first record, Philly falsetto, Dev Hynes — into the Brazilian corner Andrew kept returning to (Donato, Elis, Chico, Milton — 1971–75, the golden MPB years under the dictatorship), then the porch-and-bedroom folk of Ferrell, Ward, Chan Marshall and José González's band, and out through post-classical piano, Icelandic strings and Eno's 2:12 of glass.

Selection method

Pool = the seed artist of every Discovery card (not the suggested albums — the brief was the artists Andrew himself chose). Filter 1: mood — only artists whose catalog has a genuinely hushed register; skipped the dance/punk/EDM cards outright. Filter 2: variety — max one track per artist, and one per card, so no artist explored twice (Meshell, Santigold, Harmonia, Isleys) doubles up. Filter 3: unheard first — for each artist, the pick was a lesser-played cut rather than the card's seed track (Cat Power's Werewolf over The Greatest; Withers' Hope She'll Be Happier over Ain't No Sunshine; Elis's Atrás da Porta over Águas de Março). Versions verified by year/duration via search: original-album pressings throughout (Donato is the 1975 Lugar Comum title track, not the 2017 compilation cut). Heard-vs-new checked by bare Spotify ID before build: 19 of 25 new (76%); anchors = Air Alone in Kyoto (×10), Milton Ponta de Areia (×2), Badu Orange Moon (×1), Salvant Ghost Song (×1), Sigur Rós Starálfur (×1), Eno Emerald and Stone (×6). Rotation: global follows last night's classical — rule B satisfied.

Connective tissue & sequencing

Five movements, each a hand-off rather than a jump. I — strings without words (1–4): harp → viol consort → piano trio → vibes-and-piano; the Dowland "falling tear" motif and Lonely Woman share a downward-sighing melodic shape. II — modal East, then dub (5–8): Brahem's maqam, Mulatu's pentatonic tezeta, Nusrat's Sufi refrain over Massive Attack's bassline, and Bonobo/Rhye as the electronic landing pad. III — quiet storm (9–13): Alone in Kyoto is the pivot (French, instrumental, but built like a soul ballad), then Badu's seven-minute Orange Moon sets the tempo for Withers, the Delfonics and Blood Orange's Augustine — sixty years of falsetto and Fender Rhodes. IV — Brazil, 1971–75 (14–17): Donato's Rhodes answers Hynes's; Elis and Chico are both singing Chico's own tunes (Atrás da Porta is a Buarque/Hime song); Milton's Ponta de Areia is the one true anchor the whole set orbits. V — porch to glass (18–25): waltz-time folk (Ferrell), Ward's fingerpicked hush, Cat Power, Junip's motorik lullaby, Salvant covering Kate Bush, Rani's felted piano, Sigur Rós's strings, and Eno/Hopkins/Abrahams to close at 2:12 — the shortest track, so it ends on a held breath rather than a fade.
