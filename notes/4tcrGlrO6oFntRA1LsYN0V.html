The world

Tokyo, 1988–2001: the pop made by people who worked in — or lived inside — Shibuya's import record shops. Shibuya-kei was never a sound so much as a method: Burt Bacharach and Roger Nichols soft pop, Jobim/Gilberto bossa nova, Gainsbourg and yé-yé, Swinging-London mod, the arch Britpop of él Records, Pet Sounds harmony and Northern soul, all reassembled with a crate-digger's literacy into brand-new, impossibly stylish songs. Its two poles were Pizzicato Five (Yasuharu Konishi writing for Maki Nomiya's voice) and Flipper's Guitar (Keigo Oyamada + Kenji Ozawa), whose 1991 split produced the scene's two great solo arcs — Cornelius and Ozawa Kenji. Around them: the small labels (Oyamada's Trattoria, Konishi's Readymade, Crue-L) and the majors that bankrolled them, plus a New York branch — Cibo Matto, Buffalo Daughter on the Beastie Boys' Grand Royal, Minekawa and FPM on Emperor Norton — that carried it to the US in the late 90s.

Selection method

Adjacency is by real lineage: the Flipper's family tree (Oyamada → Cornelius, and producing Kahimi Karie's Good Morning World; Ozawa → Life and the Scha Dara Parr crossover); the Pizzicato family (Takao Tajima sang on Bellissima!, then left to form Original Love — track 1 and track 5 share a voice); Towa Tei coming home from Deee-Lite to make Future Listening! inside the scene, and later bringing Haruomi Hosono — the YMO elder — and Kylie Minogue to it; Takako Minekawa (later Oyamada's wife) and Buffalo Daughter as the US-label branch; Cymbals and FPM as the generation that carried the sound to 2001. Connective tissue & sequencing

  • Prologue (1): 誘惑について — 1988, before the word existed: Konishi's Bacharach-and-bossa blueprint with Tajima singing it.
  • The name arrives (2–4): Flipper's Guitar's neo-acoustic 恋とマシンガン (Camera Talk, 1990), P5's 東京は夜の七時 (Dec 1993 — "it's 7 pm in Tokyo", the single that made Nomiya the scene's voice), then Flipper's baggy, Primal-Scream-era Groove Tube (1991).
  • Tajima's second act → the Ozawa axis (5–7): Original Love's 接吻 (1993) — the voice from track 1 back as city soul; Scha Dara Parr with Ozawa on 今夜はブギー・バック (1994), the hip-hop crossover that took the scene to the charts; Ozawa alone on ラブリー (Life, 1994), the sunniest record of the year.
  • Bossa-tech and soul revue (8–9): Towa Tei's Technova (Bebel Gilberto's voice — Rio via New York via Tokyo) → P5's スウィート・ソウル・レヴュー (Apr 1993).
  • Whisper and the New York branch (10–13): Kahimi's Good Morning World (1995, produced by Oyamada), Sugar Water (the anchor — Honda and Hatori, two Tokyo expats in Manhattan), ベイビィ・ポータブル・ロック (Romantique 96), Minekawa's Fantastic Cat — the kitten-soft, toy-synth side of the scene.
  • Fantasma (14–15): Cornelius's 1997 pair in album order — the noise-pop jolt of Count Five or Six, then the long surf-and-sampler glide of Star Fruits Surf Rider: the moment Shibuya-kei became world-class studio pop.
  • The orbit widens (16–18): Elastic Girl (Trattoria, 1995 — Kahimi's own lyric, a nod to The Fall), Buffalo Daughter's Great Five Lakes (Neil Young sampled, Grand Royal, 1998), GBI — Towa Tei with Kylie Minogue and Haruomi Hosono, the elder blessing the children.
  • Closing (19–21): ハッピー・サッド (1997, P5 at their most bittersweet) → Cymbals' Highway Star, Speed Star (2000, the power-pop generation) → FPM's Beautiful Days (Jan 2001): the year P5 played their last show in Shibuya, closing the era on a sunrise.
