The world

The piano chair in hard bop, 1955–66: the decade when the sidemen who had held that chair for Miles, Blakey, Mingus and Coltrane stepped out as leaders on Blue Note (Alfred Lion, Rudy Van Gelder's Hackensack living room and then Englewood Cliffs), Riverside (Orrin Keepnews) and Prestige (Bob Weinstock), with a West Coast wing in Los Angeles on Contemporary, Dootone and Pacific Jazz. It runs from Bud Powell's bebop line through Horace Silver's gospel-blues funk to the church-piano soul jazz of Timmons and McCann — the music that made "Moanin'", "This Here" and "The 'In' Crowd" jukebox hits — and it is kept on labels as texture, not a filter, because the sitting itself ranged from Contemporary to Impulse!.

Connective tissue & sequencing

Chronological, 1955 → 1966, in four moves. The chair is set (1955–56): The Preacher — the tune Alfred Lion reportedly wanted to cut as too old-fashioned, which instead named the gospel streak in hard bop — then Hawes in Los Angeles, Herbie Nichols (the room's eccentric, with Max Roach at the drums), Carl Perkins (whose "Grooveyard" the Montgomery Brothers were playing in the sitting; he died in 1958 at 29) and Señor Blues. Prestige and Blue Note, 1957–58: Flanagan in Stockholm with Elvin Jones, Garland's trio, Cool Struttin' with the Miles rhythm section plus Farmer and McLean, and Bud Powell — the father of the chair — recorded at Van Gelder's three weeks after Clark's "Gee Baby" session. The soul-jazz boom, 1959–60: Hope, Kelly's trio, then the gospel run — This Here (written for Cannonball's band, and Timmons' own hit), Redd's score for The Living Theatre's The Connection, Parlan's "Us Three" (a left hand built around a polio-damaged right), Barry Harris live in San Francisco, Les McCann's 3/4 church waltz, Drew's "Undercurrent" with Hubbard and Mobley. Coda (1963–66): Tyner's Satin Doll as the ballad breather, Pearson's Sweet Honey Bee (Blue Note's house pianist/A&R man, the last session in the window), and — out of strict date order on purpose — Ramsey Lewis live at the Bohemian Caverns, where the room acquires an audience and the chair goes to the pop charts. Opens on The Preacher because it is both the founding document and the sitting's own Silver anchor; closes on The "In" Crowd because by 1965 the piano room literally had a crowd in it.
