The world

Takoma Park, Maryland, 1959: a 20-year-old John Fahey presses about a hundred copies of Blind Joe Death and names a record label after his hometown. "American Primitive" was his own term, borrowed from the painters — take the pre-war fingerpicking of Charley Patton, Sam McGee and Elizabeth Cotten and run it, alone on a steel-string guitar, through Bartók, Indian raga and the Episcopal hymnal. Takoma's orbit (Basho, Kottke, Bola Sete) forks into Windham Hill in the late seventies, and after Fahey's death in 2001 a second generation — Jack Rose in Philadelphia, Glenn Jones in Boston, Tompkins Square's Imaginational Anthem series — rebuilds the idiom from the 78s up, so that by 2025 its most vital voices are a Welsh astrophysicist in Brighton and a Virginian who lays her guitar flat across her lap. Ninety-three minutes of guitar and not one singer.

Selection method

Adjacency by label and by hand, never Spotify-similar. Labels: Takoma (Fahey, Basho, Kottke, Bola Sete) → Windham Hill (Basho's Visions of the Country, on the imprint Fahey's disciple Will Ackerman founded) → the revival imprints: Tompkins Square (Tyler's Behold the Spirit and every Gwenifer Raymond record; its Imaginational Anthem series takes its title from Takoma alumnus Max Ochs), Thrill Jockey (Glenn Jones), VDSQ (Sarah Louise — the solo-guitar label run by Fahey's biographer Steve Lowenthal), Paradise of Bachelors (Fussell). People: Basho was Fahey's college friend in DC; Kottke mailed Fahey the tape that became Takoma's best-seller; Bola Sete was Vince Guaraldi's guitarist before Fahey put out Ocean; Glenn Jones made The Epiphany of Glenn Jones with Fahey in 1997 and wrote the reissue liner notes; William Tyler produced Fussell's debut; Nathan Salsburg is Fussell's road partner (and Joan Shelley's husband — 14 plays). Sources: the three pre-war dialects the Takoma players learned from 78s — Opry ragtime (McGee), Texas slide-and-hum (Johnson), Piedmont alternating bass (Cotten). One rule kept the set honest as a voice study: solo steel-string guitar only — no bands, no singers (Johnson's hum is wordless), so Basho's vocal sides, the Cul de Sac collaboration and Bola Sete's Bettina (the 1971 band LP Andrew actually played) were passed over for solo pieces.

Connective tissue & sequencing

Chronological — the history of one instrument's voice, oldest recording to newest:

  • Sources (1926–58): Buck Dancer's Choice → Dark Was the Night, Cold Was the Ground → Wilson Rag. Three ways of making a steel-string sing alone: a Grand Ole Opry rag, a Dallas street preacher's slide with a hum where the words should be (the piece Ry Cooder rebuilt as Paris, Texas — 13 plays), and Elizabeth Cotten's upside-down left-handed alternating bass, recorded in the Seegers' living room.
  • Takoma (1963–78): Sunflower River Blues (Patton's Mississippi, Fahey's second LP) → The Grail and the Lotus (Basho's raga on 12-string) → Requiem for John Hurt (Hurt died Nov 1966; Fahey's elegy) → The Yellow Princess → Vaseline Machine Gun (Kottke's demo-tape record, the label's half-million seller) → Let Go (Bola Sete's Ocean sessions, the Takoma record Fahey never stopped praising) → Rocky Mountain Raga (Basho on Windham Hill: where the idiom forks toward the new-age it would later disown).
  • Revival (2005–15): Kensington Blues (Jack Rose's Philadelphia — the spark of the second generation) → Even to Win Is to Fail (Glenn Jones, Fahey's late collaborator) → Affirmed (Salsburg's Louisville) → Missionary Ridge (Tyler's Tennessee, on Tompkins Square) → The Golden Hour (Anderson's Portland) → The Day Is Past and Gone (Sarah Louise's variations on a Sacred Harp hymn — back to the hymnal Fahey mined for In Christ There Is No East or West).
  • Now (2021–25): Juvenescence (Yasmin Williams, lap-tapping, from the DC suburbs twenty miles from Takoma Park) → Banjo Players of Aleph One → Frolic (Fussell, Sep 19) → Long Pond Lily (Pedigo's Amarillo) → Jack Parsons Blues → Bliws Afon Tâf.
  • Opens with the oldest side (1926) because the whole idiom is an argument with the pre-war 78. Closes on Bliws Afon Tâf — Welsh for "Taff River Blues" — so the set ends where it began: a river blues, sixty-two years and an ocean from Sunflower River Blues, played by the dormant love it was built to wake.
