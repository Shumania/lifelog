The world

Coach House Studios, Clifton, Bristol — the room where Massive Attack cut Blue Lines in 1990–91 while a 19-year-old trainee tape-op named Geoff Barrow watched, and where he then recorded much of Dummy three years later. The sound that came out of that room had two parents: the Wild Bunch sound system (Smith & Mighty's 1988 Bacharach covers on Three Stripe are the proto-records; Nellee Hooper and 3D built Neneh Cherry's "Manchild" the next year) and the 4AD hush — Elizabeth Fraser, whose voice on This Mortal Coil's 1983 "Song to the Siren" is the ur-text for every half-whispered trip-hop vocal that followed, and who ended up singing on Mezzanine and on Craig Armstrong's Melankolic debut. Between 1994 and 1998 the city produced Dummy, Protection, Maxinquaye, Crustation's Bloom and Mezzanine; the rest of the map is the wave that broke beyond Bristol (Mono, Lamb, Sneaker Pimps, Mo' Wax) and the singers who still carry the hush thirty years on — Gibbons alone, FKA twigs, Sevdaliza, Smerz.

Connective tissue & sequencing

Chronological, 1983 → 2024, ~105 min.

  • The siren and the sound system (1983–90): Fraser's "Song to the Siren" (This Mortal Coil, 4AD, 1983 — Robin Guthrie on guitar) → Smith & Mighty's "Walk On" (Three Stripe, 1988 — Bacharach & David slowed to a Bristol crawl, Jackie Jackson singing) → "Manchild" (1989 — Hooper's beat, Del Naja's rap, finished with Cameron McVey) → "Cherry-coloured Funk" (Heaven or Las Vegas, 4AD, 1990). The hush and the bass meet.
  • Coach House (1991–95): "Safe From Harm" (Shara Nelson, Blue Lines, April 1991) → Dummy twice — "Roads", the stillest thing in the genre, then "Glory Box" → Tricky's "Hell Is Round the Corner" (Feb 1995), built on the same Isaac Hayes "Ike's Rap II" backing track as "Glory Box": two Bristol records from one Stax loop in a single winter → "Hyperballad" (Post, June 1995), co-produced by Nellee Hooper, with Tricky elsewhere on the album — the Wild Bunch's reach into Björk's London years.
  • The wave beyond Bristol (1996–98): "Life in Mono" (Oct 1996 — the John Barry Ipcress File cimbalom) → Lamb's "Gorecki" (Manchester, 1996 — named for the Third Symphony it samples) → "6 Underground" (Barry again: "Golden Girl" from Goldfinger; the two Barry samples sit either side of Lamb on purpose) → Crustation's "Flame" (Bristol, Jive, 1997 — Bronagh Slevin) → "This Love" (Armstrong + Fraser, Melankolic, 1998 — Armstrong had arranged "Protection" four years earlier) → "Inertia Creeps" (Mezzanine, April 1998 — the album Fraser sang on) → "Rabbit in Your Headlights" (UNKLE / DJ Shadow + Thom Yorke, Mo' Wax, 1998 — the bridge back to the sitting's Radiohead and Smile plays).
  • The voices, afterwards (2002–24): "Mysteries" (Gibbons with Paul Webb of Talk Talk as Rustin Man, 2002) → "Paradise Circus" (Heligoland, Feb 2010 — Hope Sandoval) → "Two Weeks" (FKA twigs, LP1, 2014) → "Marilyn Monroe" (Sevdaliza, Rotterdam, Children of Silk EP, Nov 2015) → "Believer" (Smerz, Oslo, XL, Feb 2021 — the Nordic edge the sitting's Toxe and Sassy 009 sparks were pointing at) → "Reaching Out" (Lives Outgrown, Domino, May 2024).
  • Opens on Fraser's siren — the voice the whole genre learned to whisper from — and closes on Beth Gibbons thirty years after Dummy, with her own name alone on the record.
