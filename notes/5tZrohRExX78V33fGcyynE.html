The world

Sinister Grift is Noah Lennox's Lisbon record — cut in his home studio with Josh "Deakin" Dibb co-producing and all three of his Animal Collective bandmates contributing, plus two guests who steer it: Spirit of the Beehive's Rivka Ravede on "Anywhere but Here" and Cindy Lee (Patrick Flegel, ex-Women) playing the guitar solo that closes "Defense". It is the Panda Bear thesis in its most band-like form: Beach Boys harmony stacks and a slight reggae/dub sway carrying words that reviewers heard as the aftermath of a long relationship ending — dark lyrics over bright harmony. That contradiction — sadness sung in major keys, falsettos stacked like a choir, reverb as weather — is what this playlist collects: the songs of the last fifteen-odd years (Baltimore and Lisbon, Philadelphia, Atlanta, Brooklyn, L.A., Long Island, the Netherlands, New Zealand via a Tokyo hotel room, Montreal, Toronto, Canada's prairies) that speak Brian Wilson's harmonic language as a first language, with Wilson's own "'Til I Die" (Surf's Up, 1971) as the root they all grow from.

Connective tissue & sequencing

  • Opens on "Anywhere but Here" — the brightest door into Sinister Grift, with Rivka Ravede's voice on it — and the next track hands the lead to her own band ("Let the Virgin Drive", Ravede on lead vocals, 2024). The Animal Collective family follows: Avey Tare's "Saturdays (Again)" (2019), the group's "Bluish" (Merriweather Post Pavilion, 2009), and the co-producer's own hushed "Golden Chords" (Deakin, 2016).
  • 2009–10 cousins: Lennox guests on Atlas Sound's "Walkabout" (Logos, 2009); Bradford Cox's Deerhunter answers with "Helicopter" (Halcyon Digest, 4AD, 2010); Grizzly Bear's "Two Weeks" is the era's most explicit Beach Boys moment.
  • L.A. canyon ring: Foxygen's "San Francisco" (2013) → "Homage" (2016) → Drugdealer & Bollinger (2022) → "Andromeda" (2019) → Pratt's "Life Is" (2024) — the sunlit-sad centre of the sequence.
  • Craftsmen abroad: the Lemon Twigs (2024) → Jacco Gardner (2013) → Connan Mockasin (2013) → UMO's "Hunnybee" (2018) → Men I Trust (2018) → Alvvays' "Dreams Tonite" (2017) — the same harmonic language travelling.
  • Closes back in Lisbon: "Ferry Lady" (the record's reggae-leaning single) into "Defense", whose closing guitar solo is Cindy Lee's — so the next voice is Cindy Lee's own, on Freak Heat Waves' "In a Moment Divine" (Mood Hut, 2023). Last word to the source: "'Til I Die", Brian Wilson's 1971 hymn of smallness — the song every falsetto here is quoting.
