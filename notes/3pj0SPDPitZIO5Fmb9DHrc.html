The world

Falsetto — head voice — is the dance floor's lead instrument: a register that sounds ecstatic and exposed at once, and that electronic music has returned to over and over since Sylvester sang "You Make Me Feel (Mighty Real)" over Patrick Cowley's synthesizers in San Francisco in 1978. This set follows that one voice chronologically through the music's turning points: vocoder-and-falsetto boogie (Earth, Wind & Fire, 1981), the UK synth-pop of Bronski Beat (1984), Jamie Principle's uncredited whisper on the Chicago house cornerstone "Your Love" (Trax, 1987), the electro-pop and nu-disco revivals of the 2000s (Zoot Woman, Scissor Sisters, Junior Boys), the 2008 crest when half of indie went to the disco (Hercules & Love Affair, Hot Chip, Friendly Fires, Passion Pit, MGMT, Empire Of The Sun), and the 2010s, when the high male voice became pop-dance's default lead (Jai Paul, Sam Smith on "Latch", Jungle, Caribou, Shamir, Franc Moody, Toro y Moi). Not a scene, not a year — a voice. 91 minutes.

Selection method

Inclusion required (a) a male lead sung in falsetto, head voice or countertenor, (b) an electronic or dance production, (c) corroborated standing — reviews, canon lists, chart history, web-verified dates — never Spotify-similar, (d) one recording per song and one song per artist. Adjacency by real lineage: Cowley's sequencers → Principle and Knuckles' drum machine → DFA's Hercules & Love Affair, whose "Blind" was remixed by Frankie Knuckles himself (both the remix and the dub are in Andrew's likes); Stuart Price's Zoot Woman (2001, four years before he produced Madonna's Confessions on a Dance Floor) opens the UK electro-pop decade that Hot Chip and Friendly Fires closed; XL Recordings signed Jai Paul, Jungle and Shamir. Connective tissue & sequencing

  • Opens on Sylvester because everything here descends from it: a song that began as a mid-tempo gospel piano number until Patrick Cowley's synthesizers turned it into the record Hi-NRG grew from (Step II, Oct 1978).
  • Roots, 1978→1987 (1–4): Sylvester → Philip Bailey's falsetto over Maurice White's vocoder on "Let's Groove" (Raise!, 1981) → Jimmy Somerville's falsetto carrying a story of leaving home ("Smalltown Boy", 1984) → Jamie Principle whispering, uncredited, over Frankie Knuckles' machine on "Your Love" (Trax, 1987). Disco → boogie → synth-pop → house in four records, one voice.
  • The 2000s revival (5–7): Zoot Woman's "Living in a Magazine" (2001) → Jake Shears' Bee Gees-styled Pink Floyd (recorded 2003, a hit in 2004; the band never cleared it) → Junior Boys' whispered Hamilton, Ontario electro-pop ("In the Morning", So This Is Goodbye, Domino, Aug 2006).
  • The 2008 crest (8–12), in release order: "Electric Feel" (Oracular Spectacular, Oct 2, 2007) → "Ready for the Floor" (single, Jan 28, 2008) → "Blind" (DFA, Mar 3, 2008 — Antony Hegarty) → "Paris" (with Au Revoir Simone, single Nov 10, 2008) → "Sleepyhead" (Chunk of Change EP, 2008; reissued on Manners, 2009). Five falsettos in thirteen months; this is the year the seed came from.
  • The 2010s (13–20): "O.N.E." (Odd Blood, 2010) → "BTSTU" (XL, Apr 21, 2011; recorded 2007) → "Latch" (PMR, Oct 8, 2012) → "Busy Earnin'" (XL, Jul 14, 2014) → "Can't Do Without You" (Our Love, City Slang, Oct 6, 2014) → "Call It Off" (Ratchet, XL, 2015 — a countertenor, not a falsetto: the one voice here that lives in head register full-time) → "Dopamine" (2017 single, Dance Moves EP 2018) → "Ordinary Pleasure" (Outer Peace, Carpark, Jan 18, 2019). Latch → Busy Earnin' → Can't Do Without You is the 2012–14 stretch where the register became the default.
  • Closes on "Walking On A Dream" (BLOND:ISH remix, Jul 2025) — the seed kept for last: the whole set walks toward the song that started it, in the reading Andrew actually played eleven times.
  • The inversion: falsetto has run through three recent builds as a hush — Vespers' whisper-R&B, Call You Rose's sweet soul, Low Sun's harmony-pop. Here it is the same instrument at the opposite register of use: full volume, on the floor.
