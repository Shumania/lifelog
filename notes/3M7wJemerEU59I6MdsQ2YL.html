The world

1967 was the year the two halves of French pop stopped pretending they lived in different cities. On one side the Salut les copains generation had grown teeth — Dutronc's deadpan garage, Polnareff's falsetto, Nino Ferrer's R&B, and a wave of one-EP mademoiselles (Jacqueline Taïeb, Clothilde, Delphine) whose fuzz-and-harpsichord sides were cut by the same Paris session men who played for the grown-ups. On the other, the Rive Gauche — Brel, Barbara, Gréco, Aznavour, Bécaud — made some of its most durable records, and Claude François wrote the breakup song that would become "My Way". In between stood Serge Gainsbourg, whose year ran from Anna (the Karina TV musical, January) through the Mr. Gainsbourg EP (July) to the Bardot affair and the Bardot Show (filmed November), and Françoise Hardy, whose Ma jeunesse fout le camp LP (November) was the first time she sounded entirely her own. Vogue, Philips, Barclay, Riviera, Disc'AZ: half a dozen labels, one city, twelve months.

Connective tissue & sequencing

Three rings, played as one day. It opens hushed with Hardy's "Voilà" — the door, the heard side — then Anna Karina's "Sous le soleil exactement" (January, Gainsbourg's TV musical) as the year's first morning. Taïeb's "7 heures du matin" is literally the alarm clock, and the beat ring runs from there: Dutronc, Nino Ferrer's telephone, the two Swinging-Mademoiselle cult sides (Clothilde — Germinal Tenas's fuzz-and-harpsichord production — and Delphine), then Vartan's "Comme un garçon" and Polnareff's "Âme câline" as the chart peaks. The Gainsbourg axis is the hinge: Bardot's "Harley Davidson" straight into Serge's own "Comic Strip" — the same affair, two sides of the same autumn — before Laforêt's Russian-doll folk-pop, Nicoletta's "La musique" and Fugain's "Je n'aurai pas le temps" let the tempo down. Hardy's title song marks the turn to evening and the grandees take the second half in rising weight: Aznavour's "Emmenez-moi" (the boat out), Bécaud, Claude François's "Comme d'habitude" (the year's most consequential song, heard here before Sinatra got to it), Gréco's "Déshabillez-moi", Barbara's letter to her audience, and Brel's "La chanson des vieux amants" as the long night. It closes where the seed sitting began: Hardy's "Des ronds dans l'eau" (the Lelouch Vivre pour vivre theme, late '67) into Delon's whispered "Laetitia" — the heard side, two and a half minutes of a man reading a letter over François de Roubaix — the last thing you hear in 1967. ~72 min.
