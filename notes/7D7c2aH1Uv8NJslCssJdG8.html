The world

The European piano trio in the two decades after Keith Jarrett's Standards Trio stopped being the only model: roughly 1998–2019, Stockholm to Zurich to Manchester. Esbjörn Svensson's trio (Svensson / Dan Berglund / Magnus Öström) is the hinge — a jazz trio that toured like a rock band, played through effects, wrote hooks, and sold out rooms to people who owned no other jazz records — and when Svensson drowned off Ingarö in June 2008 the scene he had opened kept going without him. The labels are the map: ECM for the hushed Nordic end (Stenson, Gustavsen, Wasilewski, Bärtsch), ACT in Munich for the e.s.t. orbit (Rantala, Wollny, Öström), Gondwana and Edition in Britain for the trios that grew up on electronica (GoGo Penguin, Mammal Hands, Phronesis). Brooklyn's Dawn of Midi arrived at the same place from minimalism rather than jazz, which is why they close the argument.

Connective tissue & sequencing

  • Prologue (1–2): Mehldau's Exit Music (1998) is the American proof that a piano trio could take a Radiohead song seriously; From Gagarin's Point of View (1999, live) is the Swedish answer — the tune that made e.s.t.'s name.
  • The Nordic hush (3–7): Stenson's Oleo de mujer con sombrero (Silvio Rodríguez, ECM 1998) → Gustavsen's Tears Transforming (2005) → Wasilewski's January (2008) → Helge Lien's Natsukashii (2011). Then Iiro Rantala's Tears for Esbjörn (2011): the first of two elegies, placed right before Svensson himself comes back in.
  • The e.s.t. orbit at full tilt (8–11): Dodge the Dodo live in Montreux is the trio at its most rock; Tingvall's Vägen and Wollny's Nachtfahrten are the German-Swedish trios that took that energy on; Cowley's Siren's Last Look Back is the London version.
  • Britain after electronica (12–14): Phronesis Urban Control → GoGo Penguin Hopopono → Mammal Hands Boreal Forest — the Edition/Gondwana trios where the drummer is thinking about drum & bass.
  • Ritual groove (15–16): Bärtsch's Modul 42 (Zurich, ECM 2008) and Dawn of Midi's Atlas (Brooklyn, 2013): acoustic trios playing interlocking loops by hand — the anchor-to-new pairing that ties Andrew's Dysnomia sitting to the European line.
  • Coda (17–18): Öström's Ballad for E (with Pat Metheny) is the drummer's own farewell to Svensson; closes on Rymden's Bergen (2019) — Öström and Berglund with Bugge Wesseltoft, the surviving two-thirds of e.s.t. starting again as a trio. The playlist ends where the band did not.
