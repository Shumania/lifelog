The world

Michel Legrand's songbook, 1958–1977. A Paris Conservatoire kid who flew to New York in June 1958 and, at 26, conducted Miles Davis, Coltrane and a 28-year-old Bill Evans on Legrand Jazz; who then scored Jacques Demy's sung-through films — Les Parapluies de Cherbourg (1964) and Les Demoiselles de Rochefort (1967) — and moved to Hollywood, where The Thomas Crown Affair (1968, Oscar for "The Windmills of Your Mind"), The Happy Ending (1969) and Summer of '42 (1971, Oscar for the score) followed. Each French film melody was handed an English lyric — Norman Gimbel for the Cherbourg songs, Johnny Mercer for "Once Upon a Summertime," Alan & Marilyn Bergman for nearly everything after — and each one was absorbed by jazz musicians within a few years of the film. This is that hand-off, song by song: the origin version beside the readings that turned it into a standard.

Connective tissue & sequencing

  • Prologue — the room where it starts: "Django" from Legrand Jazz (NYC, June 25, 1958) — Legrand conducting Miles, Coltrane and Bill Evans. Nineteen years later Evans records Legrand's song as an album title; the whole playlist lives between those two dates.
  • I. La Valse des Lilas → Once Upon a Summertime: Legrand sings his own 1954 waltz (Chante et s'accompagne, 1964) → Blossom Dearie, the first English recording (1958) → Zetterlund & Evans in Stockholm (Waltz for Debby, 1964), the first time Evans played Legrand on record.
  • II. Les Parapluies de Cherbourg: "Le récit de Cassard" (1964), the melody Norman Gimbel made "Watch What Happens" → Tony Bennett's early English vocal (1965) → Vince Guaraldi on Alma-Ville (1969), his final studio album.
  • III. The Gil Evans pair, plus a reprise: Miles's muted "Once Upon a Summertime" (Quiet Nights) → Astrud's "I Will Wait for You" — the other Cherbourg song — both arranged by Gil Evans → Alison Balsom's trumpet returns to "La Valse des Lilas" fifty years on, the trumpet-to-trumpet hinge into the American film years.
  • IV. The Windmills of Your Mind: Noel Harrison's Oscar-winning original (The Thomas Crown Affair, 1968) → Dusty in Memphis (1969) → Dorothy Ashby's harp on Dorothy's Harp (Cadet, 1969) → Legrand himself in French, "Les moulins de mon cœur" (1970).
  • V–VI. The Bergman years: Evans's electric-piano "What Are You Doing the Rest of Your Life?" (From Left to Right, 1970) → Sarah Vaughan with Legrand conducting (1972) → Sinatra's "The Summer Knows" (1974), the one song here in a single reading, as the pivot into the finale.
  • VII. Maxence: Jacques Revaux's original "Chanson de Maxence" from Les Demoiselles de Rochefort (1967) → Tony Bennett & Bill Evans, Together Again (1977) → closes on Evans's trio "You Must Believe in Spring" (recorded August 1977, released 1981) — Sunday morning's record, given the last word, now with the song's whole history in front of it.
