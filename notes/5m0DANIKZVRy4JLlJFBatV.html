The world

American jazz in the thirty years after 1996, when players who grew up with MTV and a Blue Note collection stopped treating the Great American Songbook as a closed book. Hancock's all-star date (Brecker, Scofield, Holland, DeJohnette) made the argument from above; then the players a generation younger — Mehldau, The Bad Plus, Jason Moran, Dave Douglas, Vijay Iyer, Robert Glasper — made it their ordinary working repertoire, on Blue Note, Nonesuch, Columbia and ACT. The room where much of it started is Largo, the Los Angeles club where Jon Brion held a Friday residency and Mehldau first heard Elliott Smith; the 2002 album named after it and this year's Ride into the Sun are the two ends of one thought. The songbook itself is narrow and specific: Nirvana, Radiohead, Björk, Pavement, Beck, Aphex Twin, PJ Harvey, late Bowie, Elliott Smith.

Selection method

Connective tissue & sequencing

Chronological, because the order is the argument.

  • The first wave, Nirvana as repertoire (1–2): Hancock's "All Apologies" (1996) opens — the namesake, and a pianist of the old songbook generation taking Cobain seriously two years after his death. Charlie Hunter's trio had already slipped "Come As You Are" onto a Blue Note debut in 1995.
  • The next generation (3–6): Moran's "Joga" (Björk, with Tarus Mateen and Nasheet Waits) → Mehldau's "Paranoid Android" from Largo, the Jon Brion production → Douglas's "Poses" (Rufus Wainwright, another Largo regular) → The Bad Plus's "Flim", an Aphex Twin miniature played straight by an acoustic trio, recorded by Tchad Blake.
  • Outside the jazz clubhouse (7–10): O'Riley, a classical pianist, transcribing Radiohead ("Motion Picture Soundtrack", the dormant-love anchor); Dr. Lonnie Smith and David "Fathead" Newman giving Beck's "Tropicalia" the organ-combo treatment; Wasilewski's ECM trio finding "Hyperballad" (Spotify lists Björk first in the credits — she is the composer; the recording is the trio's); and four mainstream heavyweights — James Carter, Cyrus Chestnut, Reginald Veal, Ali Jackson — playing Pavement's "Here".
  • Second nature (11–15): Glasper folds Radiohead into Hancock's own "Maiden Voyage", which closes the loop back to track 1; Benevento's "Heartbeats" (The Knife) and Iyer's "Galang" (M.I.A.) widen the book past guitar bands; Scott's "The Eraser" (Thom Yorke) and Yaron Herman's "Heart Shaped Box" return to the two main wells.
  • Slow songs and the Bowie band (16–19): Ben Allison's "Missed" (PJ Harvey, Michael Blake's tenor carrying the lyric) → Redman's "Doll Is Mine" (Blonde Redhead) from Walking Shadows, the ballads album Mehldau produced and plays piano on → Donny McCaslin's two-minute "54 Cymru Beats" (Aphex Twin again, released on Dave Douglas's Greenleaf label) with Lindner, Lefebvre and Guiliana, the quartet that went on to make Blackstar with David Bowie → Guiliana's own quartet closing Jersey with Bowie's "Where Are We Now?".
  • Closes on Mehldau's "Between the Bars" (2025): the week's seed, and the pianist from track 4 arriving, twenty-three years later, at the songwriter he first heard at Largo.
