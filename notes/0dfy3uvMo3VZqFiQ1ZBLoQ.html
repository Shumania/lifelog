The world

The East Neuk of Fife — Anstruther, Crail, Cellardyke, St Andrews — between 1997 and 2014. In 1997 two things happened at once: The Beta Band — Steve Mason and Gordon Anderson's band — released Champion Versions in London, and back home Gordon's brother Kenny, the record shop he worked in having gone bust, started Fence Records to sell his friends' music (Gordon, sent home by ill health, was one of the first, recording as Lone Pigeon). Fence grew from a folk-slanted CD-R operation into a collective — James Yorkston, KT Tunstall, U.N.P.O.C., The Pictish Trail (Johnny Lynch, who ran it from 2003), Lone Pigeon, Withered Hand, Kid Canaveral, Rozi Plain, Frànçois & The Atlas Mountains — with its own festivals in the harbour towns: Homegame, Haarfest, Hott Loggz and Eye o' the Dug. The sound is home-recorded and unhurried: accordion and acoustic guitar with drum machines and tape hiss, sea-fog melancholy with a wry grin. The collective closed shop in August 2013 (Lynch took the diaspora to Lost Map); Kenny's From Scotland With Love the next year is the coda. Vic Galloway wrote the book — Songs in the Key of Fife — and this is the mixtape for it.

Connective tissue & sequencing

Five branches of one tree, 95 minutes, roughly in the order the family left home and came back.

  • The Beta Band, 1997–2001 (1–2): Dry the Rain — the song that made them, co-written by Gordon Anderson, whom ill health sent home to Fife soon after the band formed (he was recording as Lone Pigeon on Fence by 1997) — then Squares, the Hot Shots II single (the one you liked).
  • Steve Mason's aliases (3–5): I Walk the Earth from the King Biscuit Time No Style EP (2000, the alias he kept alive inside the Beta Band — last night's record); Boys Outside (2010), his first album under his own name, produced by Richard X; The People Say (2023, Domino) to show the line still runs.
  • Gordon's branch — The Aliens (6–7): after the Beta Band split in 2004, Robin Jones and John Maclean asked Gordon Anderson to front their new band. Robot Man (which already existed as a Lone Pigeon home recording) and Setting Sun, both from Astronomy for Dogs (EMI, 2007) — the sunniest psychedelia in this whole story, from the brother who'd been sent home in 1997.
  • Home to Fence, 2002–2012 (8–16): Kenny's 678 (KC Rules OK, 2005 — the first King Creosote record most people heard) opens the gate; then the collective in loose chronology — U.N.P.O.C.'s Here On My Own (Tom Bauchop, Fifth Column, Domino 2003), Yorkston & The Athletes' St. Patrick (Moving Up Country, 2002), KT Tunstall's Throw Me a Rope (her debut EP False Alarm, 2004 — months before Eye to the Telescope turned the Fence regular into a pop star; she'd lived with Gordon Anderson in the Fence days and later wrote "Funnyman" about him), Pictish Trail's Winter Home Disco (Secret Soundz Vol. 1, 2008 — Johnny Lynch, who ran the label), Withered Hand's Religious Songs (Good News, 2009 — Dan Willson, a Fence Collective regular whose split CD-R with King Creosote, On Behalf of My Wife and I, came out on the label), Kid Canaveral's Good Morning (2010; the St Andrews band Fence signed and reissued in 2011), then the Bristol arrivals who went on to Domino and Lost Map — Frànçois & The Atlas Mountains' Les Plus Beaux (E Volo Love, 2012; their Plaine Inondable came out on Fence in 2009 — the one French lyric here) and Rozi Plain's Jogalong (Friend, Lost Map 2015; her debut was a 2008 Fence CD).
  • Jon Hopkins walks in (17–21): the 2007 Woozy With Cider remix is Hopkins working with Yorkston four years before Diamond Mine — it's the hinge, the electronics arriving from London into the folk room. Then Bubble (the anchor from last night), Honest Words (the Diamond Mine single that didn't make the original seven), For One Night Only (From Scotland With Love, 2014 — Kenny scoring a century of archive footage for the Glasgow Commonwealth Games, the year after Fence closed), and the close: Immunity (Asleep Version) — Kenny Anderson's voice over Hopkins' piano, the last track of Hopkins' record, slowed down further for the Asleep Versions EP. Opens on the song the two brothers' worlds split over; closes on the one where Fife and London finally sing together.
