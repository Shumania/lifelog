The world

LUX is built on borrowed voices — Estrella Morente and Sílvia Pérez Cruz on La Rumba del Perdón, Carminho on Memória, Yahritza y Su Esencia on La Perla — and this is the room those voices come from: cante jondo as it was remade from the 1970s on (Camarón with Paco de Lucía, Lole y Manuel, Enrique Morente in Granada), the Catalan, Basque and Portuguese singers who were quietly making art-song out of their own languages in the same years (Serrat, Laboa, Amália), and the generation Rosalía came up with — Pérez Cruz, María José Llergo, Niño de Elche, Raül Refree, who produced her 2017 debut. It is a mood comp, not a lineage map: one feeling — the hushed, close-miked Iberian voice with a guitar or strings behind it — followed across the Atlantic to Cabo Verde, Veracruz and Yakima.

Connective tissue & sequencing

  • The LUX credits as doors: Reliquia opens — the track that came back in April. Sílvia Pérez Cruz's Vestida de Nit (the habanera her parents wrote: lyrics Glòria Cruz, music Càstor Pérez) leads into La Rumba del Perdón, where she and Estrella Morente sing with Rosalía; Estrella's own Volver (Gardel and Le Pera's 1934 tango turned flamenco for Almodóvar's 2006 film, lip-synced by Penélope Cruz) hands to her father.
  • The Lorca thread: Enrique Morente's Pequeño Vals Vienés (OMEGA, 1996, with Lagartija Nick — Lorca's poem, the one Cohen turned into "Take This Waltz", sung back in Spanish) → Camarón's Nana del Caballo Grande (La Leyenda del Tiempo, 1979 — the lullaby from Blood Wedding) → Lole y Manuel's Tu Mirá (1975; Tarantino put it in Kill Bill Vol. 2) — the seventies records that made cante modern — → De Madrugá, Rosalía's own 104 seconds of palmas and cante.
  • Rosalía's generation: María José Llergo (Pozoblanco, Córdoba) — her Nana del Mediterráneo answers Camarón's nana — and Un Veneno, C. Tangana (who wrote lyrics on eight of El Mal Querer's eleven songs) with Niño de Elche.
  • The other languages, before Franco died: Serrat's Paraules d'amor (Com ho fa el vent, 1968, Catalan) and Mikel Laboa's Txoria txori (Joxean Artze's poem; recorded for Bat-Hiru, 1974, Basque) — then Amália's Estranha forma de vida (Asas fechadas, 1962; her own lyric over Alfredo Marceneiro's melody) opens the Portuguese room.
  • Fado, 1962 → 2025: Amália → Memória (Carminho wrote it over the old "Fado Maria Rita") → Carminho's own 2009 debut (Escrevi teu nome no vento) → Lina_Raül Refree's Gaivota (2020 — Amália's songbook rewired by the producer of Rosalía's Los Ángeles) → Madredeus' O Pastor (Existir, 1990, Teresa Salgueiro singing).
  • Across the water: Sodade (Miss Perfumado, 1992) is saudade as morna; Natalia Lafourcade's Amor Clandestino (Cancionera, 2025) brings the cantaor Israel Fernández into her Mexican songbook; Yahritza y Su Esencia (Yakima, Washington — the trio on La Perla; Soy El Único was written at fourteen) and Silvana Estrada's Te Guardo (Marchita, 2022) are the Mexican hush.
  • Closes on Magnolias — the last song on LUX, a song about dying peacefully. 85 min.
