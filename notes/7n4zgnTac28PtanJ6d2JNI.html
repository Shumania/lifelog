The world

The sad banger: the pop mode of the last fifteen years in which the dancefloor is where you go to cry. Robyn's "Dancing On My Own" (Konichiwa, 2010) is its founding text — "I'm in the corner, watching you kiss her" over a four-on-the-floor that never lets up — and everything here descends from that bargain: four-to-the-floor or a hard synth pulse, a chorus built to be shouted, and a lyric about longing, humiliation, jealousy, or the morning after. It runs from the 2012–13 indie-synth underground (Sky Ferreira's Dev Hynes/Rechtshaid single, Grimes' Montreal bedroom-pop, Tegan and Sara's Greg Kurstin pivot) through the Scandinavian and British mid-decade (Tove Lo, Shura, CHVRCHES, Sigrid, Georgia) to Lorde's Melodrama as the thesis statement, and ends in the 2024–26 crest where it became the dominant pop language: Chappell Roan, the Charli/Lorde reconciliation, Lorde's Virgin, MUNA and Lykke Li this spring. Mostly women-led; all of it English-language; none of it hushed.

Connective tissue & sequencing

Chronological, because the mood has a history and the sequence tells it:

  • 2010–13, the underground finds the shape: Robyn → Sky Ferreira (the 2012 single that made heartbreak-synth-pop an indie currency) → Grimes' "Oblivion" (fear, bounced) → Tegan and Sara's "Closer", the indie band going pop on purpose with Kurstin.
  • 2013–16, Scandinavia and Britain: Tove Lo's "Habits" (dinner in the bathtub), Allie X's "Catch" (heartbreak as illness), CHVRCHES' "Clearest Blue" (the build-and-drop as the physical shape of waiting), Carly Rae's "Run Away With Me" (the sax, the plea), Christine and the Queens' "Tilted", Shura's "What's It Gonna Be?".
  • 2017, the thesis: "Green Light" in the exact middle — Lorde and Antonoff's post-breakup sprint, the song that made this mode the sound of serious pop. Sigrid's "Strangers" is its Nordic echo; Mitski's "Nobody" the indie version — a disco song about having no one.
  • 2019–22, maximal: Polachek (Nigro), Georgia's literal "About Work the Dancefloor", Dua Lipa's "Don't Start Now" (the break-up disco hit), Japanese Breakfast's "Be Sweet" and Rina Sawayama's "Hold the Girl" (joy and healing at the same tempo).
  • 2024–26, the crest: the Charli/Lorde remix — two women working out a rivalry on a 130-bpm A. G. Cook track — hands directly to Chappell's "Good Luck, Babe!" (Nigro) and Lorde's "What Was That" (her return, Jim-E Stack), then this spring's two records: MUNA's title track ("heartbreak into kinetic euphoria") and Lykke Li's "Lucky Again".
  • Opens on the founding text, in the corner. Closes on "Lucky Again" — 4 a.m., one last euphoric dance before the hangover, from an album Lykke Li has called her last. 85 minutes.
