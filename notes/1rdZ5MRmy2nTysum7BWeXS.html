Seeds

The page credits exactly three pieces: Philip Glass's "Elizabeth Chooses a Career" and "Death of the Twins/Finale" (© 1996 Dunvagen, credited to Glass / Jean Cocteau / Susan Marshall) and Thomas Newman's "End Title" from Little Children (New Line, 2006). This is a show-prep list, deliberately short — three pieces, nothing added, built with the explicit-ask --allow-short exception.

The world

Petal is Helen Pickett's 2008 ballet, commissioned by Aspen Santa Fe Ballet and premiered in Santa Fe on February 1, 2008 — a bright, saturated piece (Pickett designed the set herself) that ABT took into its repertory in 2026 through the New Works Initiative. Its score is a splice of two American film-and-stage composers writing a decade apart. The Glass pieces come from Les Enfants Terribles (1996), his "dance opera" for three pianos and four voices after Cocteau's 1929 novel, made with choreographer Susan Marshall as the last panel of his Cocteau trilogy (Orphée, La Belle et la Bête, Les Enfants Terribles). The Newman piece is the closing cue of Todd Field's Little Children (2006) — suburban dread resolved into a long, warm exhale, Newman's signature prepared-piano-and-strings palette at its most patient.

Selection method

Credits-only: the three titles printed in the program, no adjacency, no padding. Recording choice mattered for Glass. The credited titles — "Elizabeth Chooses a Career" and "Death of the Twins/Finale" — are the movement names of the Les Enfants Terribles suite for piano four hands (Michael Riesman's arrangement), not the scene names of the full opera (the 2005 Orange Mountain cast recording labels the same music "Scene 9: Call Me Elisabeth" and "Scene 20: Paul's End"). The Namekawa / Dennis Russell Davies recording on Reich & Glass: Music 4 Hands (Orange Mountain, 2005) is the only Spotify release carrying the credited titles verbatim, so it is the one used — and the piano-duet texture is what you hear in the theatre. Newman's "End Title (Little Children)" is the 7:41 closer of the original score album. Heard-vs-new verified by bare track ID pre-build: 3 of 3 new; anchors = none.

Connective tissue & sequencing

Program order, which is also the ballet's order: Glass's two piano movements first — the quick, bright-edged "Elizabeth Chooses a Career", then the darker arpeggio engine of "Death of the Twins/Finale" — and Newman's "End Title" as the long cool-down. The thread is two composers who share a toolkit (repeating piano figures, harmonic patience, film-scoring instincts) and even an awards season: in early 2007 Newman (The Good German) and Glass (Notes on a Scandal) were nominated side by side for Best Original Score. Opens on the piece the ballet opens on; closes on the only cue that was written to be an ending.
