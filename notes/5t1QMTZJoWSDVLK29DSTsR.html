The world

Sixty years of composers and players treating the Baroque as raw material rather than scripture. It starts in Paris and Stuttgart in 1959–65, when Jacques Loussier's trio, Eugen Cicero and the Swingle Singers discovered that a Bach line already swings; runs through the Modern Jazz Quartet's Blues on Bach (1973), Arvo Pärt's Collage über B-A-C-H (1964) and Michael Nyman's Purcell grounds for The Draughtsman's Contract (1982); passes through the piano Goldbergs of Uri Caine and Dan Tepfer; and lands in the 2010s Deutsche Grammophon "Recomposed" era — Richter's Vivaldi, Peter Gregson's cello suites, Ólafsson's Bach Reworks with Reykjavík's Bedroom Community producers (Valgeir Sigurðsson, Ben Frost) and Ryuichi Sakamoto. Nonesuch's After Bach and Bach Trios sit in the same room. The common gesture: keep the score, change the hands.

Connective tissue & sequencing

  • Jazz Bach, 1959–73: Loussier's Air → the Swingles' Art of Fugue → Cicero's C.P.E. Bach Solfeggio → MJQ's Blues in H (B) — the whole first wave, four flavours of the same discovery.
  • Modernist hinge: Pärt's Collage (the B-A-C-H motif torn and re-glued) → Nyman's Purcell ground — the moment recomposition stops being a jazz novelty and becomes composition.
  • The Goldberg thread: Caine's Aria → Tepfer's Aria... → Mehldau's WTC prelude and his own Pastorale answer — three pianists, three distances from the page, ending on the record Andrew already knows.
  • Vivaldi's Seasons, twice: Richter's Spring 1 and Summer 3 (the anchors) → Meredith's Haze (Summer) → Pluhar's Purcell ground on the same string-band footing.
  • String-band Bach: Wachet auf (Ma/Thile/Meyer) → Thile's solo mandolin Presto → Gregson's recomposed cello Prelude and Sarabande — the cello suites arriving through mandolin first.
  • Reykjavík reworks: Ólafsson's own Prelude in G → Valgeir's rework → Ben Frost's Ladder Mix (the electronic peak) → Richter's Shadow 1 as a drone landing → Sakamoto's BWV 974 Adagio rework.
  • Opens with Loussier's Air on a G String — the first Bach anyone rebuilt for a jazz trio, and the gentlest door in. Closes on the Sakamoto rework: Marcello's oboe adagio, transcribed by Bach, recorded by Ólafsson, reworked by Sakamoto — a recomposition of a recomposition of a recomposition, which is the whole playlist in one track.
