The world

Sixty-five years of the desert instrumental: a music with no singer where the guitar does the talking and the reverb is the landscape. It starts in 1959–60 AM-radio twang (Duane Eddy's Phoenix studio, Santo & Johnny's steel, The Shadows' Apache — a desert fantasy recorded in London), gets mythologized by Morricone's spaghetti westerns, turns literal American geography with Ry Cooder's Paris, Texas slide, becomes a self-aware scene in 1990s Tucson (Calexico, Friends of Dean Martinez — bands that shared members and a zip code), drifts through the American-primitive/ambient-country school (William Tyler, Marisa Anderson, SUSS), and lands in today's Latin-psych instrumental revival — Hermanos Gutiérrez, Glass Beams, BALTHVS, Los Bitchos, Money Chicha — which is where Andrew's August listening already lives.

Connective tissue & sequencing

Chronological arc — the history of the sound, oldest to newest:

  • Twang origins (1959–60): Apache → Sleep Walk → Forty Miles of Bad Road — melody-first, tremolo and steel.
  • The myth (1966–68): The Ecstasy of Gold → Man with a Harmonica — Morricone turns twang into cinema.
  • The literal desert (1985–98): Paris, Texas → All the Pretty Horses → Minas de Cobre — slide guitar as landscape, then the Tucson school makes it a scene.
  • The meditative school (1998–2019): Cadillac Desert → Into the Light → Ursa Major → De Ushuaia a la Quiaca — American-primitive and ambient-country stretch it out; Santaolalla's ronroco bridges to Latin America.
  • The revival (2016–24): Los Ojos Del Cóndor → Mirage → Sun Colored Eyes → Las Panteras → Echo en Mexico → Phantom Feelings — the current wave, with the biggest anchor (23 plays) opening the block.
