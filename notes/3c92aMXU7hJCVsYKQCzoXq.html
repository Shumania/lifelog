The world

Country music in 1971 — the year Nashville's establishment swallowed the new songwriters whole. Kristofferson's songs sat at #1 in other people's mouths all winter (Janis's posthumous pop #1 in March, Sammi Smith's crossover #1 the same month, Waylon's "The Taker" at #5 country), his own second LP arrived in July, and behind him came the rest of the new writers: John Prine, signed to Atlantic by Jerry Wexler after Kris pulled him onstage at the Bitter End; Mickey Newbury on Elektra; Townes Van Zandt on Poppy; Tom T. Hall telling the truth about Clayton Delaney on Mercury. Meanwhile the hitmakers had one of their best years — Loretta's two #1s, Dolly's "Coat of Many Colors", Charley Pride's signature song, Haggard's eleventh #1, Twitty, Wynette, Paycheck's Epic debut, Cash's "Man in Black" — cut in the same rooms (RCA Studio B, Quadrafonic, the Columbia and Decca studios; American Sound in Memphis for Prine; Los Angeles for Pearl and Townes). The frame is strict: one recording per song, released in 1971, sequenced in the order the year heard them.

Connective tissue & sequencing

Loosely chronological, January to December. It opens with Janis's "Me and Bobby McGee" — out January 11, 1971, #1 pop that March, the record that put Kristofferson's name in every mouth — and Sammi Smith's whisper of "Help Me Make It Through the Night", #1 country the same month. Spring is the establishment at full stride: Jerry Reed hot, Conway's "How Much More Can She Stand" (a Harry Compton song), Cash's "Man in Black" (#3), then Waylon at RCA Studio B with the Kristofferson–Shel Silverstein "The Taker". Summer: Wynette's Billy Sherrill side (#1 Aug 28), Tom T. Hall's "Clayton Delaney" (#1 Sep 18), Joan Baez at Quadrafonic with Norbert Putnam and the Nashville cats turning The Band's song into a #3 pop hit, and then Kris himself — The Silver Tongued Devil and I dropped in July, and its four cuts are split 2+2 across the back half because the LP sat on the chart from July into 1972. Autumn is the harvest: Paycheck's Epic debut (the Jerry Williams Jr. / Gary U.S. Bonds song), Loretta's "You're Lookin' at Country", Dolly's coat (cut April 27, out in September), Newbury's "An American Trilogy", Prine's "Sam Stone" (Kris's line about having to break his thumbs), Pride's "Kiss an Angel Good Mornin'" (#1), Haggard's "Carolyn" (a Tommy Collins song, his eleventh #1 in January '72), Townes's "To Live Is to Fly". It closes with Loretta's "One's on the Way" — the Nov-1971 single, #1 in February — a second Shel Silverstein song to rhyme with "The Taker", and the year ending on a laugh in a kitchen in Topeka. The threads: Kristofferson sung by others (1, 2, 6) against Kristofferson sung by Kristofferson (10, 11, 16, 19); Silverstein at both ends; and the new writers (8, 15, 17, 21) threaded between the hits rather than quarantined from them, which is how 1971 actually sounded.
