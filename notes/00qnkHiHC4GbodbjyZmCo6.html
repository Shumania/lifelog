The world

This is the songwriter's room rather than any one scene: women who write pen-first, where the lyric is the event and the arrangement gets out of the way. It begins in the mid-70s with two kinds of family band — Christine McVie folding hymn-plain ballads into Fleetwood Mac's California machine, and the sister acts of the Northeast folk circuit: the McGarrigles in Montreal writing bilingual parlour songs about weather and leaving, and the Roches, three sisters from New Jersey whose 1979 debut Robert Fripp produced with "audio vérité" restraint — no drums, harmonies so close they beat against each other. The 90s move to Chicago and Atlanta: Liz Phair's deadpan Guyville reports and Chan Marshall's Moon Pix, recorded in Melbourne with the Dirty Three, where the songs barely hold together and that is the point. The 2000s are New York — Regina Spektor's Soviet-émigré piano tales, Joan Wasser's slow-burn soul after years as Antony's and Rufus Wainwright's violinist, Karen O putting the Yeah Yeah Yeahs' howl away for a ukulele — plus Jenny Lewis carrying the Laurel Canyon torch out of Rilo Kiley. And it ends in the golden-hour present: Kacey Musgraves' cosmic country, Sierra Ferrell's West Virginia tent-show Americana, Phoebe Bridgers' whispered Pasadena confessionals and Skye Newman, a 21-year-old from Camden whose 2025 breakout is the youngest voice in the deck.

Selection method

Strict double filter: (1) only artists with a Discovery card of their own — no adjacent artists, no label-mates; (2) only tracks with zero rows in consumption_history and zero title-matches under any other Spotify ID (the bare-ID check alone called Heart Like a Wheel, Samson and Us new when Andrew had heard them under different releases — those were struck by title). Two songs per artist to give every writer a pair of angles (McVie's 1975 and 1977; Phair's Whip-Smart and Whitechocolatespaceegg; Cat Power's own song and her one-take Good Woman), one for Skye Newman whose catalogue is a year old. Candidates came from the canon core of each artist — the songs critics and the artists themselves cite — not from popularity rank, which is why Songbird (unheard here, astonishingly) sits beside I Eat Dinner. Heard-vs-new verified pre-build: 25 of 25 new (100%); no anchors by design — the "anchor" is a track he never reached on a record he loved.

Connective tissue & sequencing

Strict chronology, 1975 → 2025, so the playlist reads as one fifty-year conversation. Hidden wiring between explored cards: Fripp (card #33) produced the Roches' debut; Beck (#155) produced Jenny Lewis's The Voyager, hence Just One of the Guys; Danger Mouse of Broken Bells (#45) co-wrote Turn the Light with Karen O; Ezra Koenig duets on The Moon Song; Cat Power's Good Woman features Eddie Vedder and Warren Ellis. Movements: Sisters (1–6: McVie's two ballads open soft, the Roches' close harmony, the McGarrigles' Montreal mid-tempo) → Guyville & Moon Pix (7–10: the guitars get dry and the voices flat and truthful) → New York (11–17: Spektor's piano, Wasser's slow soul, Lewis's country-rock, Karen O's ukulele as the hinge) → Golden hour (18–25: Bridgers' whisper, Musgraves' cosmic country, Ferrell's fiddle Americana, Karen O returning with strings). Warm Ways opens because it is the quietest thing McVie ever wrote — a room-tone start. Hairdresser closes because it's the newest song by the youngest writer and the only one that walks out the door mid-sentence.
