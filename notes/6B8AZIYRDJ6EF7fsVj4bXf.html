The world

Hip-hop's jazz year, February to November 1993. Digable Planets' Reachin' opens it (Feb 9) with Art Blakey under the drums; Guru's Jazzmatazz Vol. 1 (May 18) puts Donald Byrd, Roy Ayers, Branford Marsalis, Lonnie Liston Smith and MC Solaar in the room with the rapper instead of under him; Blue Note itself signs off on the whole idea by handing Us3 the keys to its vault for Hand on the Torch; and on Nov 9 Midnight Marauders and Enter the Wu-Tang come out on the same day. Around those pillars sits the year's underground — Newark (Lords of the Underground), Queens (The Beatnuts), the Bronx (Ultramagnetic), Brooklyn (Masta Ace, Black Moon), Long Island (De La Soul, Leaders of the New School), Oakland's Hieroglyphics (Souls of Mischief, Del) and LA's Good Life Cafe / Likwit crews (Freestyle Fellowship, Tha Alkaholiks) — all cutting SP-1200 and MPC loops off Blue Note, CTI and Prestige records. This is the texture the ATCQ radio was circling: dusty, mid-tempo, horn-and-Rhodes boom bap, before G-funk and the shiny-suit era changed the sound of the decade.

Connective tissue & sequencing

Runs in release order, Feb → Nov 1993, so the year unfolds the way it happened — the shape's own logic doing the sequencing.

  • Feb–Mar: Reachin' opens — the year's opening statement and its Grammy single, Art Blakey's "Stretching" under it. Then Brand Nubian's Elektra return and Newark's Lords (Marley Marl & K-Def on the boards).
  • Apr–May, the undergrounds into Jazzmatazz: Beatnuts' Intoxicated Demons EP (Andrew already plays their '94 record), Freestyle Fellowship's Good Life Cafe polyrhythms, Masta Ace's SlaughtaHouse — then Guru with Donald Byrd playing live on "Loungin'" and MC Solaar trading verses in French on "Le Bien, Le Mal": the Paris connection, and the one bilingual moment.
  • Aug–Sep: Bronx (Ultramagnetic) and LA (Alkaholiks) lead into the fortnight of Sept 21–28 — Buhloone Mindstate ("Breakadawn" flipping Michael Jackson's "I Can't Help It"), Return of the Boom Bap (Premier's "Outta Here"), and 93 'til Infinity, whose title track (Billy Cobham's "Heather" under it) gives the playlist its name.
  • Oct: Trends of Culture, then Black Moon — Da Beatminerz flipping Donald Byrd's "Wind Parade", Byrd's second appearance here — and Busta's breakout on "What's Next".
  • Nov 9, the double release day: a triptych — "Award Tour" (Weldon Irvine) → "C.R.E.A.M." (the Charmels; the same-day neighbor from Staten Island) → "Electric Relaxation" (Ronnie Foster's "Mystic Brew", which Andrew already has six plays of).
  • Nov, the tail: Latifah's "U.N.I.T.Y." (the Crusaders' "Message from the Inner City") and Del's "Catch a Bad One".
  • Closes on "Cantaloop" — Blue Note's own record, Herbie Hancock's "Cantaloupe Island" plus Pee Wee Marquette's Birdland intro: the jazz establishment signing its name to the year the rappers spent in its vaults.
