The world

The cello of the last twenty years has become a one-player ensemble. A loop pedal and a multitrack turned a chamber instrument into a self-contained orchestra — Zoë Keating's self-released Into the Trees (2010), Julia Kent's Asperities (The Leaf Label, 2015), Anne Müller's Heliopause (Erased Tapes, 2019), Resina's Traces (130701, 2018) and Clarice Jensen's four albums for the same FatCat imprint are all records by cellists who need nobody else in the room. At the same time the instrument learned to speak in vernaculars it never had: Abel Selaocoe's Sesotho and Xhosa praise songs with the Manchester Collective, Vincent Segal's cello folded into Ballaké Sissoko's kora on Chamber Music (No Format!, 2009), Gaspar Claus's Tancade (InFiné, 2021). There is an amplified American edge — Erik Friedlander's camping-trip solo record Block Ice & Propane (2007), Seattle's Lori Goldston (Nirvana's touring cellist, 1993–94) on Creekside: Solo Cello (2014), Alison Chesley's Helen Money on Thrill Jockey, Oliver Coates's pedal-crushed skins n slime (RVNG Intl., 2020), and this May's Bill Orcutt × Mabe Fratti duet Almost Waking (Unheard of Hope) — and, holding the far end, the Icelandic drone school: Hildur Guðnadóttir's Touch-era Without Sinking (2009) and Gyða Valtýsdóttir's Epicycle II (Sono Luminus, 2020), where each track is a piece written for her by an Icelandic composer-friend.

Selection method

Adjacency by instrument, label and collaborator, never Spotify-similar. The spine is the loop-pedal lineage (Keating → Kent → Müller → Resina → Jensen, with 130701 linking Resina and Jensen directly); the second ring is cellists who play with another tradition (Selaocoe/Manchester Collective, Sissoko & Segal, Claus); the third is the amplified/improvising Americans (Friedlander, Orcutt & Fratti, Goldston, Helen Money, Coates); the fourth is Iceland, where Skúli Sverrisson appears on Hildur's Without Sinking and opens Gyða's Epicycle II. Vocal-language stamp is instrumental — the one sung track, "Kea Morata," is Selaocoe singing against his own bow, which is the point of the voice study; Hildur's "Erindi" was chosen because it is the cello-centric instrumental on an album that otherwise sings. Connective tissue & sequencing

  • I. Loops (1–7): opens on "From A to B" — the most-played Jensen track and a literal itinerary — then the pedal lineage proper: Keating's kinetic "Escape Artist," Müller's "Solo? Repeat!" (the method named in the title), Kent's multitracked "Invitation to the Voyage," Gyða's "Octo" (María Huld Markan Sigfúsdóttir's piece layers a looped cello passacaglia), Resina's "Trigger," and back to Jensen's "2,1" to close the circle on the label that houses both.
  • II. The kinetic bow (8–11): "Igiul" — Luigi backwards — is Selaocoe's reading of Sicilian cellist Giovanni Sollima's Boccherini homage L.B. Files; it hands to Segal's cello inside Sissoko's kora ("Oscarine"), to Claus's short, driving "Une foule," and lands on "Kea Morata," where Selaocoe's voice and cello are one instrument.
  • III. Amplified (12–16): Friedlander's "Night White" (solo cello as Americana) → Orcutt & Fratti's "Forced & Forced & Forced" (Pitchfork heard her cello impersonating an electric guitar and a stand-up bass; one of the album's six instrumentals) → Goldston's "Messy," the Pacific-Northwest grunge-adjacent cello Pitchfork heard echoed in the Orcutt record → Helen Money's distorted "Machine" (Become Zero, made in LA with Neurosis drummer Jason Roeder and pianist Rachel Grimes) → Coates's "Honey," where the fuzz dissolves into melody.
  • IV. North (17–20): Gyða's "Air to Breath" (Daníel Bjarnason's cello solo over a chordal ostinato — written to the instrument's likeness to the human voice) → Hildur's "Elevation" from Without Sinking → "Erindi," the shortest and barest piece here, Hildur joining a trio of viola, viola da gamba and cello recorded in Hansa's Meistersaal → closes on "Unity," the same Jensen album as the opener: twenty tracks resolving back to one instrument.
