The world

Britain, 1971–2019 — the country that turned "early music" from a scholars' hobby into a recording industry. David Munrow's shawms and nakers made it pop in 1971; then 1973 produced the founding institutions almost at once: Christopher Hogwood's Academy of Ancient Music, Trevor Pinnock's English Concert (formed late 1972), Peter Phillips's Tallis Scholars — with Anthony Rooley's Consort of Musicke and John Eliot Gardiner's Monteverdi Choir alongside, and Emma Kirkby and James Bowman as its voices. A second generation formed its own bands — The Sixteen (1979), The King's Consort (1980), McCreesh's Gabrieli Consort and Huggett's Sonnerie (both 1982), Fretwork (1985) — and a third now runs the old ones: Richard Egarr took the AAM from Hogwood in 2006, Bojan Čičić leads it (and Florilegium) today, Rachel Podger led the English Concert from 1997, John Butt's Dunedin Consort works out of Edinburgh, and Jonathan Cohen's Arcangelo and Peter Whelan's Ensemble Marsyas dig up what the founders never reached. The repertoire runs Dowland to Handel; the sound is gut strings, viols, cornetts and natural trumpet, pitched low.

Connective tissue & sequencing

Roughly chronological by recording, so the generations arrive in order:

  • Prologue (1): Munrow's Palästinalied with James Bowman — the countertenor voice of the first revival, answered nineteen tracks later by Iestyn Davies.
  • The 1973 founders (2–7): Hogwood/AAM's Boyce (an English symphony, bright) → Pinnock's Corelli (the concerto-grosso model everything after copies) → Kirkby & Rooley's Dowland (the first hush) → the Tallis Scholars' Byrd → Gardiner's Duo Seraphim live in San Marco with cornetts → McCreesh's twelve-part canzona: the same Venetian brass, next generation.
  • Viols (8–9): Fretwork twice — Dowland's Lachrimae Antiquae, then Purcell's Fantazia upon one note, where one viol holds a single middle C throughout while the other four move around it.
  • Solo violin → Vivaldi (10–12): Huggett's ten-minute Biber Passacaglia is the still centre; Podger — who began in Florilegium and led Pinnock's English Concert — answers with Vivaldi's Stravaganza; the King's/AAM Gloria brings the chorus back.
  • Handel three ways (13–15): Sampson's German aria (hushed, with obbligato) → Dunedin's Acis Sinfonia (the 1718 Cannons version) → Balsom's natural trumpet with Pinnock — Handel at home, in Edinburgh, and at court.
  • Rediscoveries (16–19): Florilegium's Telemann (Čičić's other band) → Čičić's Carbonelli (principal violin of Handel's Haymarket opera orchestra, later the King's wine merchant) → Marsyas's Barsanti (Lucca → the Edinburgh Musical Society, Op. 3 published there in 1742) → Arcangelo's Buxtehude trio sonata — the third generation's appetite for what the founders never recorded.
  • Coda (20–22): Davies & Fretwork's O Solitude → The Sixteen's Hear my prayer (eight voices, one long-held dissonance) → Egarr's Brandenburg 6 Adagio, the record Andrew chose yesterday, heard now as the end of a fifty-year line rather than the start of one.
