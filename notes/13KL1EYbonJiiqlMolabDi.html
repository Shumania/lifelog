The world

West Africa's oil-boom decade, heard as one scene with three addresses. Lagos: Fela back from London and Los Angeles, inventing Afrobeat with drummer Tony Allen at the Shrine and inside the Kalakuta Republic; a few streets away, Ginger Baker's ARC studio (opened 1973) turning a generation of Baker alumni — BLO, Monomono's Joni Haastrup — and schoolboys like Ofege into Afro-rock, while the Lijadu twins (Fela's cousins) cut Danger and William Onyeabor wired up his own synth studio out in Enugu. Accra: the Uhuru Dance Band alumni (Ebo Taylor, Gyedu-Blay Ambolley, Oscar Sulley's Bukom Mashie — Bukom being the city's boxing quarter), Marijata and K. Frimpong's Cubano Fiestas bending highlife into funk. London: where Ebo Taylor and Fela had both been music students in the early sixties, where Ghanaian émigrés Osibisa gave the world the word "Afro-rock" in 1971 and Caribbean Londoners Cymande answered a year later, and where — decades on — Kokoroko learned the whole thing back off these records.

Selection method

Adjacency by bandstand and studio, never Spotify-similar: the Afrika 70 orbit (Fela → Tony Allen's own LPs → the Lijadu cousins), the ARC/Ginger Baker alumni network (BLO, Monomono, Ofege with BLO's Berkley Jones on lead guitar, the Funkees who left Aba for London in 1973), Masekela's 1973 Lagos session with Accra's Hedzoleh Soundz as the literal bridge between the two cities, the Uhuru Dance Band family tree on the Ghana side, and the London bookends chosen because they are the same scene's émigré wing (Osibisa, Cymande) and its heirs (Kokoroko cite Ebo Taylor, Fela and Tony Allen by name; Rejoice is Allen and Masekela together, London 2010). Reissue provenance was checked — every 1970s track is a period master (Strut/Soundway/Mr Bongo/Luaka Bop), not a re-recording; Ambolley's 2017 re-cut of Simigwa-Do was passed over for the 1975 This Hustling World.

Connective tissue & sequencing

  • London prologue (1971–72): Music for Gong Gong → Bra — the two records that sold Afro-rock and Afro-funk to Britain, a year apart in the same city. The playlist starts where the word started.
  • Lagos, first pass: Orlando Julius (Alo Mi Alo), the Afro-soul elder Fela learned from → Roforofo Fight, the Shrine at full length → Segun Bucknor's three-minute La La La as the breather after the epic.
  • The ARC kids: Monomono → BLO → Ofege → the Funkees — Ginger Baker's Lagos alumni and their schoolboy protégés, the fuzz-and-organ wing of the same scene, ending on the Igbo chant of Akula Owu Onyeara.
  • The sisters, then the outlier: Danger (1976) → Orere-Elejigbo (1979), the Lijadu twins' hard and sweet sides back to back → Onyeabor's Better Change Your Mind, the decade's self-built synth-funk answer from Enugu.
  • The drummer and the pivot: Tony Allen steps out front on Jealousy → Water No Get Enemy, Fela's hushed masterpiece, slows the room and turns it toward Ghana.
  • Lagos → Accra bridge: Languta — Masekela recording Accra's Hedzoleh Soundz in Lagos in 1973, the one track that lives in both cities.
  • Accra: Bukom Mashie → K. Frimpong's Kyenkyen Bi Adi M'awu → Ambolley's This Hustling World → Ebo Taylor's Heaven → Marijata's No Condition Is Permanent → Will You Promise, the Sep 14 seed, landing exactly where the Uhuru family tree says it should.
  • London coda, forty years on: Robbers, Thugs and Muggers — Fela's drummer and Masekela's horn, the two elders of this playlist together at last → closes on Abusey Junction, Kokoroko's Ebo-Taylor-shaped sunset: the young Londoners Andrew was playing on Sep 19, closing the loop on the records they learned from.
