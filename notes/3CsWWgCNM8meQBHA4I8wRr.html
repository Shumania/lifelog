The world

London, 2008 — the year the press settled on "nu-folk" for a circle of twenty-year-olds who had spent 2006–07 playing to each other at the Bosun's Locker, a basement beneath a pasty shop on the King's Road in Fulham, and at the Communion club night in Notting Hill. In twelve months the circle released everything that defined it: Laura Marling's Alas, I Cannot Swim (4 Feb, produced by Charlie Fink of Noah and the Whale, her boyfriend at the time), Johnny Flynn & the Sussex Wit's A Larum (26 May), Noah and the Whale's Peaceful, the World Lays Me Down (11 Aug), and Mumford & Sons' two Chess Club EPs (Lend Me Your Eyes, August; Love Your Ground, 3 Nov) — with Marling singing backing vocals in Noah and the Whale until she left that year to go solo, Marcus Mumford drumming in Marling's touring band, and Fink producing Jay Jay Pistolet's Happy Birthday You EP. Around that core, the fellow travellers who shared the stages: Emmy the Great (London anti-folk; We Almost Had a Baby, 10 Nov, the first single from First Love), Dev Hynes's Lightspeed Champion (Falling Off the Lavender Bridge, 21 Jan — recorded in Omaha with Mike Mogis, Emmy on backing vocals across the record), teenage Alessi's Ark (The Horse EP, her first for Virgin, also Mogis-produced), Moshi Moshi's Wave Pictures (Instant Coffee Baby, 5 May), and — down in Brighton — the Willkommen Collective's Sons of Noel and Adrian and the Leisure Society, whose debut single The Last of the Melting Snow arrived on 15 December and earned an Ivor Novello nomination the following spring. Mumford's 2008 EPs aren't on Spotify, so the band is present here only as the drummer in Marling's room.

Connective tissue & sequencing

  • Opens with The Box — the first song on A Larum, i.e. the next thing Andrew would hear if he kept going — then Ghosts, the first song on Alas: the year's two debuts, back to back.
  • The Marling web (3–6): Young Love, the March single with Marling guesting — the scene's first crossover moment → 2 Atoms in a Molecule, Marling as Noah and the Whale's backing singer → Jay Jay Pistolet, Marling's friend and Fink's production client → My Manic and I, Marling alone.
  • Spring to summer (7–11): Flynn's Leftovers, the Wave Pictures' May LP, then 5 Years Time — the August anthem and the scene's one real hit — dropping to Alessi's Ark's two-minute The Horse and back to Flynn's Eyeless in Holloway, the most London song on his record.
  • The anti-folk wing (12–15): Night Terror into Lightspeed Champion ×2 bracketing Emmy the Great's November single — Emmy sings on the Hynes record, so the three sit as one household.
  • Closing arc (16–19): Tickle Me Pink, the last burst of energy; Failure, Marling turning the lights down; Indigo from Brighton's Willkommen collective; and it closes on The Last of the Melting Snow — released 15 December 2008, the year's literal last word. 64 minutes.
