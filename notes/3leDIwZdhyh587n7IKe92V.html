The world

Instrumental guitar trios and duos, 2015–2026, playing a borderless groove music with the reverb turned up: BALTHVS (Bogotá) and Khruangbin (Houston) on the psychedelic-funk side, Hermanos Gutiérrez's two-guitar desert westerns, LA LOM's Los Angeles cumbia and bolero, Arc De Soleil's sun-bleached surf-funk, Glass Beams out of Melbourne. Two elders anchor it: Tommy Guerrero, the San Francisco skateboarder whose guitar instrumentals go back to 1997's Loose Grooves and Bastard Blues, and Vieux Farka Touré, whose Ali (2022, with Khruangbin) re-plays his father Ali Farka Touré's songs. Adrian Quesada's Boleros Psicodélicos II and Jaguar Sound are the Austin wing. Mostly instrumental; where there is a voice (the Ali tracks, a few Khruangbin cuts) it sits inside the band rather than in front of it.

Connective tissue & sequencing

The order is the queue's own, untouched. BALTHVS is the spine — every fifth track, five different releases from MACROCOSM (2020) to Manifest (2026), five of the ten tracks from Harvest (2024). Between those posts the same company comes round about every eleven tracks — Hermanos Gutiérrez, LA LOM, Arc De Soleil, Khruangbin, Vieux Farka Touré, Tommy Guerrero — with the gaps filled by one- and two-track visitors (KATZROAR, Adrian Quesada, Five and Tens, Surprise Chef, Glass Beams, Monster Rally, Yuuf, Richard Houghten). Collaborations stitch the neighbours together: Quesada's "Primos" features Hermanos Gutiérrez (9), all four Ali tracks are Vieux Farka Touré with Khruangbin (7, 18, 29, 40), and "Tres Hermanos" brings in Dan Auerbach (47). It opens on "Mango Season" and closes on Khruangbin's "Hold Me Up (Thank You)" because that is where the queue began and ended.
