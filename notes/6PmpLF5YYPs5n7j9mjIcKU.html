Eight cards, one bassline.

The world

Punk-funk is what happened when post-punk kids in Manchester, New York, Leeds and Bristol stopped pretending they didn't love disco and Parliament. "Shack Up" is the Rosetta stone — a Banbarra cover done in trench coats on Factory Records, all scratchy rhythm guitar and a bassline that won't quit. The same year, downtown New York was doing it with more chaos (James White, Bush Tetras) and, at 99 Records, more purity (ESG, Liquid Liquid — sampled by half of hip-hop). Bristol's Pop Group and Maximum Joy brought dub and politics; Pigbag and 23 Skidoo went horn-heavy and percussive; Tom Tom Club made it a pop hit. The Factory strain never died: Quando Quango's Mike Pickering DJ'd the Haçienda, where Happy Mondays and the Stone Roses turned the groove baggy in 1988–89, and Black Grape carried it into the Britpop years. New York's DFA label rebooted the whole thing in 2002 — the Rapture's "House of Jealous Lovers" is basically ESG with a cowbell budget — and the current UK crop (Shopping, Warmduscher, Fat Dog, supermodel*, Getdown Services) treats the same rhythm section as a comedy stage and a dancefloor at once.

Selection method

Lineage-map shape: four generations of one rhythm, chosen by scene, label and lineage rather than any "similar artists" feed. Every 1979–83 pick is a documented node — 99 Records (ESG, Liquid Liquid), ZE (James White), Y/Rough Trade Bristol (Pop Group, Maximum Joy), Factory (ACR, Quando Quango), Leeds (Gang of Four), plus Pigbag and 23 Skidoo as the horn/percussion wing. Quando Quango, ESG's Come Away, Black Grape and Fat Dog came straight off the Discovery cards' own suggestion lists. Where a heard track existed I took the sibling: "Dance" instead of "Moody" for ESG, "Optimo" instead of "Cavern," "Wrote for Luck" instead of "Step On," "Fools Gold" (liked, never played) instead of "I Wanna Be Adored." Heard-vs-new verified by bare track ID pre-build: 18 of 24 new (75%); anchors = Shack Up, Genius of Love, Disparate Youth, Dog Dribble, plus supermodel*'s "i used to live in england" (one play) and the liked-only Fools Gold. Versions: the 12" mixes for Pigbag, Maximum Joy and 23 Skidoo are the canonical club cuts, and James White's "Contort Yourself" is the August Darnell ZE 12" — the one everybody means.

Connective tissue & sequencing

Strict chronology, because the point is watching one idea travel. Opens on the anchor, then the 1979 New York/Bristol originals it grew up beside, through 99 Records' purest groove into Tom Tom Club's pop crossover — the first time this sound reached a radio. The Pigbag → Maximum Joy → Knife Slits Water → 23 Skidoo → Quando Quango stretch is the long-form 12" middle, the most dancefloor part of the record, and it ends inside the Haçienda, so Happy Mondays and Fools Gold arrive as a natural next night out. Black Grape hands off to the Rapture across a seven-year gap that sounds like none at all; !!! and Santigold are the 2000s proving the bassline survived indie-disco. The last five are the current UK generation, and it closes on "Dog Dribble" — Getdown Services being the band that most obviously heard "Shack Up" and decided it was also funny. Runtime is long (~2 h) thanks to the 12" versions; that's deliberate, it's a groove record, not a singles set.
