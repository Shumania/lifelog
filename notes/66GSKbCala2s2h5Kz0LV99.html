Seeds

Andrew, Sunday 2026-11-01 10:34 AM: "make a new garage burn — try to focus on less songs I've heard before, not necessarily zero, but just only 5 to 10%. Whenever it's ready, play it in the garage and replace whatever's playing there now." The four earlier volumes (7-2026, 8-2026, Vol. 3, Vol. 4 — 96 tracks, 66 lead artists) are built on hits everyone knows: Toxic, One More Time, Uptown Funk, Sandstorm. This one keeps the same workout arc and swaps the hits for the records the DJs in those scenes actually play. Two anchors came straight from his history: Alan Braxe & Fred Falke's Intro (played twice, last Sep 2) and Jessie Ware's Free Yourself (twice, last Mar 14). Nothing else on the list has a play, a like, or a Shazam anywhere in the archive, and no track repeats an earlier volume.

The world

Four rooms of the same club, in the order you'd walk through them. The front bar is modern funk and nu-disco — Tuxedo (Mayer Hawthorne + Jake One), Chromeo, London's Franc Moody, Ed Banger's Myd, Róisín Murphy's Róisín Machine. The main floor is house, from the 1998 Soulsearcher disco-house cut and Alan Braxe's 2000 French-touch instrumental through to the 2022–24 UK and Chicago wave: Honey Dijon, Jamie xx, Barry Can't Swim, Four Tet's KH alias, Mella Dee, Caribou's Honey. The back room is the electro / techno peak — Overmono, Joy Orbison, Floating Points' Birth4000, Boys Noize, and Vitalic's 2005 Poney Part 1, the track that defined "French electro" before Justice. And the exit is big beat and indie-electro — Mylo, the Australian pair The Presets and Cut Copy (Modular Records, 2008), Propellerheads and Apollo 440 from the 1997–98 Wall of Sound / Skint moment that Fatboy Slim and the Chemical Brothers (volumes 1–4) grew out of.

Selection method

Started from the four existing volumes and asked, for each of their four phases, "who are the deeper names in that exact scene?" — labels (Ed Banger, Modular, Wall of Sound, Skint, Defected/Glitterbox, XL), collaborators (Honey Dijon appears both on her own and with Jamie xx; Four Tet's KH alias next to Floating Points), and DJ-set staples rather than radio hits. Preferred Spotify popularity in the 30s–50s over the 70s–80s so that "unheard by the archive" also means "probably unheard in life"; passed on Parcels' Tieduprightnow, Le Tigre's Deceptacon, ANOTR's Relax My Eyes and Kylie's Padam Padam for exactly that reason, and on Jungle because the archive has 33 Jungle plays. Mau P's Drugs From Amsterdam only surfaced as compilation copies, so it stayed out (Rule 30 spirit). Heard-vs-new verified by bare track ID pre-build: 23 of 25 new (92%); anchors = Free Yourself (track 5) and Intro (track 7). Artists already on an earlier volume (Daft Punk, Purple Disco Machine, Chemical Brothers, Fatboy Slim, Dom Dolla, Dua Lipa…) were excluded outright. One recording per song; radio edits chosen where the full mix runs past six minutes.

Connective tissue & sequencing

Same four-phase shape as volumes 3 and 4, about 100 minutes: warm-up (1–6, ~110–118 bpm funk and disco) → climb (7–15, house, with Intro as the gear change and Confidence Man's Holiday as the top of the climb) → peak (16–20, the hardest five minutes are Vitalic at 20) → finish (21–25, big-beat tempo drops to ~105–115 bpm but the energy stays up for the cool-down lap). Jessie Ware at 5 is the hinge: a disco-house track by a singer he already plays, placed where the room starts to fill. Caribou separates the two Honey Dijon records. Propellerheads' Spybreak! into Apollo 440's Stop the Rock is the 1997–98 Wall of Sound exit — the same lineage as the Fatboy Slim closers on 7-2026 and Vol. 3, one ring further out.
