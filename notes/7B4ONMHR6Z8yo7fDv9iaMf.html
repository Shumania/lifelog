The world

Kenny Barron's compositional catalogue, 1978–2025 — the small book of tunes a sideman-turned-leader kept returning to across Xanadu, Enja, Candid, Verve, Venus, Impulse! and finally Jean-Philippe Allard's Artwork Records. The settings move with his career: a solo debut recorded for Xanadu in 1981 (At the Piano), the Enja trio with Dave Holland and Daniel Humair (Scratch, 1985), the What If? quintet with Wallace Roney, John Stubblefield, Cecil McBee and Victor Lewis (1986), the Stan Getz quartet he anchored through the late '80s, the Verve years (Spirit Song, 2000), the Venus trio with George Mraz and Ben Riley (Minor Blues, 2009), and the Kitagawa/Blake trio that cut Book of Intuition (2016) and then Songbook. Ten tunes, each heard twice: the instrumental original beside what it became once someone sang it or Getz played it.

Anchors = the seven Songbook readings: Calypso (Tyreek McDole), In The Slow Lane (Kurt Elling), Minor Blues Redux (Catherine Russell, who wrote her own lyric with Paul Kahn — the album's only non-Jarrett words), Sonia Braga (Ekep Nkwelle), Cook's Bay (Ann Hampton Callaway), Sunshower and Song For Abdullah (Cécile McLorin Salvant). The other six Songbook tunes (Illusion, Lullabye, Marie Laveau, Until Then, Beyond This Place, Thoughts and Dreams) were left off to keep the card majority new; Until Then appears instead as two instrumental readings 29 years apart.

Connective tissue & sequencing

  • Opens solo: Calypso alone at the piano on his 1981 solo debut, then McDole's vocal — the smallest possible statement of the whole idea, bright enough to start a day.
  • The tune that became a standard: Voyage from What If? (1986), then Getz's live reading from the summer-1989 European tour with Barron in the band. Getz told Ted Gioia the 1986 Voyage album was the first record he ever made sober; the title tune was Barron's.
  • The Kitagawa/Blake thread: In The Slow Lane and Cook's Bay come from Book of Intuition (Impulse!, 2016) — the same trio that plays behind every singer on Songbook, so the "original" and the "cover" share a rhythm section nine years apart.
  • Blues → Brazil: Minor Blues (Venus, 2009, with Mraz and Riley) into Russell's Redux; Sonja Braga from Spirit Song (2000) — a tune carrying the name of the Brazilian actress — into Nkwelle's reading; then the samba Until Then as the 1991 Enja quintet and as the 2020 trio with Dave Holland and Johnathan Blake.
  • The dark centre: Phantoms twice — the 1986 quintet original, then the 2008 Traveler version where Gretchen Parlato sings Janice Jarrett's words: the first time Jarrett's lyrics appeared on Barron's music, and the seed of Songbook seventeen years later.
  • The best-known ballad: Sunshower — a tune Barron says emerged during a soundcheck and first recorded on Innocence (1978) — in Getz's long-shelved 1989 reading, then Salvant's.
  • Closes on Song For Abdullah, written for Abdullah Ibrahim after Barron saw him at Sweet Basil: the Holland/Humair trio version from Scratch (Enja, 1985), then Salvant's — the lead single of Songbook and the track Andrew reached for again in May. The last word goes to the song he already loves.
