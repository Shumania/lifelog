The world

Memphis is a blues town on paper, but for thirty years it ran one of the great jazz pipelines in America, and almost all of it came out of two schools. Manassas High School on the north side, where Jimmie Lunceford taught in the late 1920s and built his first orchestra out of students, produced the 1950s class that left town together — Booker Little, Frank Strozier, George Coleman, Harold Mabern, Charles Lloyd, Hank Crawford — with the Newborn family (Phineas Sr.'s Beale Street band, sons Phineas Jr. and Calvin) as the city's house virtuosi. A generation later Memphis State turned out three pianists — James Williams, Mulgrew Miller, Donald Brown — who held Art Blakey's Jazz Messengers piano chair almost back-to-back from 1977 to 1986. This is that lineage as one long set: the hometown teacher, the prodigy, the horn class that scattered to Chicago, New York and Los Angeles, and the pianists who inherited the whole thing.

Connective tissue & sequencing

  • The teacher first: Lunceford's Rhythm Is Our Business (1934) opens because the Manassas band program is literally where the pipeline starts — the swing-era polish that every kid in this list grew up around.
  • The prodigy, twice: Barbados → Cheryl — two Charlie Parker tunes at Newborn's terrifying 1956–61 peak. Cheryl is planted early on purpose: it returns at track 11.
  • The Manassas horn class (1958–66): Louis Smith's Brownie tribute (Blue Note, with Cannonball moonlighting as "Buckshot La Funke") → Booker Little's Strength and Sanity, the elegy he cut months before dying at 23 → Strozier's Runnin' with Little in the front line → Hank Crawford's The Peeper, the Ray Charles band's soul-jazz side of the same school → Lloyd's Forest Flower: Sunrise, the moment a Manassas kid became a festival headliner.
  • Breather: Phineas's Lush Life — the third anchor, placed as the still center of the set before the second generation arrives.
  • The Memphis State chair (1978–88): George Coleman's Amsterdam After Dark bridges the generations (he later hired all three younger pianists). Then the Cheryl callback — same Parker tune, 25 years on, James Williams at Blakey's piano — followed by each Messengers pianist as a leader: Williams's Alter Ego, Miller's Wingspan, Brown's Early Bird.
  • Coming home: Crawford + Calvin Newborn reunite two Manassas alumni on Breezin' (1993); Mabern, at 82, plays his own 1968 tune A Few Miles From Memphis live at Smoke — the elder statesman naming the whole playlist.
  • Closes on Phineas's Harlem Blues (1969), after the breakdowns and the hospitalizations, playing the blues form his father's band played on Beale Street — the lineage folding back to where it started.
