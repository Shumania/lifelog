The world

The soul comeback record as a genre of its own, 2002–2020. It starts with Joe Henry putting a 62-year-old Solomon Burke in front of songs by Dylan, Waits, Costello and Van Morrison for Fat Possum — a blues-punk label that had never made a soul record — and winning a Grammy. For the next two decades, indie labels did the same thing over and over: Anti- with LaVette and Mavis, Daptone building its own house band around Sharon Jones, Charles Bradley and Naomi Shelton, XL pairing Bobby Womack with Damon Albarn, Fat Possum again for Don Bryant, Dan Auerbach's Easy Eye for Robert Finley, and a reborn Stax for William Bell. The through-line is the voice itself: singers in their sixties and seventies, long dropped by the industry, recorded dry and close by producers who trusted the grain of an old instrument more than a young one. Not nostalgia — these are the records where the second act turned out to be the best act.

Connective tissue & sequencing

  • Order is chronological by comeback record — the playlist is the phenomenon unfolding, 2002 → 2020, with Bradley's Changes pulled out of sequence as a coda.
  • The Joe Henry opening (1–3): Don't Give Up On Me is the thesis statement and the record that started it all; None of Us Are Free (Brenda Russell) is the same album's sermon; Joy (Lucinda Williams) is what Henry did next with LaVette. Opens exactly where Andrew's Aug 9 afternoon did.
  • Muscle Shoals / Gulf Coast (4–6): LaVette at FAME with Drive-By Truckers; Candi Staton — the original FAME soul queen — singing Will Oldham; Irma Thomas' first record after Katrina.
  • Gospel spine (7, 10, 12): Mavis with Cooder's civil-rights songbook, Naomi Shelton's church-basement Daptone, Mavis again with Tweedy — the three most spiritual tracks spaced out as pillars.
  • Daptone / Truth & Soul heat (8, 10, 11, 13): Sharon Jones, Shelton, Lee Fields, Bradley — the Brooklyn house-band sound that turned the comeback into a scene, not a series of one-offs.
  • The Memphis-and-beyond late wave (14–19): Womack's XL pair (heard → unheard, back to back), then the 2016–17 cluster — Bell back on Stax, Rush at 82, Don Bryant with Hi Rhythm singing O.V. Wright, Finley's Auerbach debut.
  • Closes on Swamp Dogg's Auto-Tuned, Justin Vernon-haunted 2020 — the strangest and latest bloom — then Changes as coda: Bradley singing Sabbath to his mother, his last studio album before his death in 2017. The whole playlist is about voices that outlasted the industry; that one is the voice that didn't get another act, so it gets the last word.
